Part 2 · Dial sweep on model 1888

The dial across its whole range

Trained codes only sample the dials that training happened to find, nearly all between −0.2 and 0.7, and some of those are negative conductances. To see the whole dial, we take one model, checkpoint 1888, replace its trained code with a uniform ring, and step the ring's G_pol/G_ref from 0 to 2 in steps of 0.01. That is 201 runs with everything else unchanged.

A single isolated cell gives a map to compare against. Below 0.802 it has only a depolarised state. Between 0.802 and 1.439 it is bistable: it can sit in either a hyperpolarised or a depolarised state. Above 1.439 only the hyperpolarised state is left, so a tissue made of such cells would go uniformly dark. If the tissue simply followed its cells, the pattern would change at those two thresholds.

With longer holds: 301 and 500 iterations

Training used two hold durations, 100 and 301 iterations, and apart from their codes and hold lengths the 96 checkpoints are identical. Repeating the sweep with the ring held for 301 iterations therefore shows the dial as every hold-301 seed experiences it. A 500-iteration hold, which no seed was trained with, extends the trend. Nothing else changes: same 201 dials, same run length, same readout at iterations 2000–2999. A longer hold therefore also leaves less time between release and readout (1900, 1700 and 1500 iterations), a confound tested in the next subsection.

Adjusting for readout timing

With the readout fixed at iterations 2000–2999, a longer hold leaves less time between release and readout: 1900 iterations after the 100-iteration hold, 1700 after 301 and 1500 after 500. The tissue cycles on a scale of roughly 1000–1500 iterations, so some of the differences above could come from reading the cycle at a different point. The 301- and 500-iteration sweeps were therefore repeated with the run lengthened by the extra hold (3201 and 3400 iterations), so the readout covers 1900–2899 iterations after release, as for the 100-iteration hold. Up to iteration 2999 the lengthened runs are identical to the originals (checked), so only the readout window moves.

Against a clamp-free run

Every comparison so far set one hold against another. The same tissue can also be left with no clamp at all, from the same initial state, and read over exactly the iterations each sweep uses. The distance from that free run measures what a hold actually changes. Late patterns are compared over the same late window, and held states with the free state at the same iteration.

Part 4 · Clamp and pattern in one harmonic language

Does the long hold progress more smoothly?

Contrast, churn and separated parts miss something visible in the filmstrips: with the 301-iteration hold, the patterns seem to progress more smoothly as the dial turns. With the 100-iteration hold, for example, an X at dial 1.15 becomes a plus by 1.30. Harmonics give a general way to test this, and they describe clamp and pattern in the same terms. Two families of harmonics fit this tissue, and they answer different questions (see "Which harmonics?" below). A 500-iteration hold tests whether the smoothing grows with hold length, and release-aligned readouts test whether it is an artefact of when the pattern is read.

Split the lattice into concentric square shells and read each shell clockwise from its top-left corner, just as the clamp code is read around the ring. Each shell then breaks into circular harmonic orders. Order 0 is the shell's mean. Order 4 compares the shell's corners (its diagonals) with its edge midpoints (its axes): negative when the corners are more hyperpolarised, an X, and positive for a plus. Because the square lattice has four-fold symmetry, a clamp of order k can only drive pattern orders k ± 4, k ± 8, and so on. A uniform dial therefore makes only orders 0, 4, 8, …, and every dial-sweep pattern is fully symmetric: 21 numbers describe it exactly. Followed as the dial turns, those 21 numbers trace a trajectory for each hold, and its smoothness can be measured harmonic by harmonic or for the pattern as a whole.

Restricting the comparison to dials below 0.802, where a single cell has only its depolarised state, gives a different answer. Widening the range to 0–1.30 takes in the lower part of the bistable window and changes the answer again. The same measures are used for both ranges, for all five sweeps.

One visible case: X or plus

Which harmonics?

Shell harmonics are not the only choice for the interior. The tissue has harmonics of its own: the 2D cosine modes of the 11 × 11 grid. They are exactly the eigenmodes of the gap-junction coupling on a lattice without wrap-around, strictly so only while all junction conductances are equal, since the junctions are voltage-gated. Each mode is a wavevector (p, q) and describes spatial scale and direction. Shell harmonics describe radius and angle instead. Angle is the only coordinate the clamp has, so shell harmonics are what let clamp and pattern be compared.

Model and simulation · all parts

Tissue
An 11 × 11 lattice with no wrap-around, using the native Stigmergic 11 × 11 parameters: field screen size 4, field strength 1.0, transduction weight 1000, transduction bias 0.0005 and gap-junction strength 0.05. These are identical in all 96 checkpoints used.
Initial state
Uniform Vmem of −9.2 mV in every cell, the same stored condition in every checkpoint.
Boundary clamp
At each iteration of the hold, after the normal update, the polarising conductance of all 40 outer-ring cells is overwritten with its clamp value × G_ref, and Vmem is updated once more. The code is that list of clamp values, written G_pol/G_ref. After release, field transduction sets G_pol again and clips it to [0, 2] G_ref.
Clamp range
The held value is written directly and is not clipped. Training allowed clamp values in [−1, 1], a range chosen for oscillatory field signals. As a result, 87 of the 96 trained codes contain at least one negative value (23.9% of all their ring values), and 13 have a negative mean. Those held negative conductances during the hold. Every code simulated in Parts 2 and 3 lies inside [0, 2].
Single-cell regimes
For an isolated cell: below G_pol/G_ref = 0.802 there is one depolarised fixed point, at about −7.1 mV. From 0.802 to 1.439 there are two stable states, at about −50.4 and −10.0 mV, separated by a saddle at about −29.3 mV. Above 1.439 there is one hyperpolarised state, at about −52 to −53 mV.
Run
3000 iterations (dt = 0.01, so 30 time units), simulated forward only, with no training. Replays use the same procedure as §12.7's scoring scripts, which reproduced every checked checkpoint's stored training loss to within 0.0012.
Readout
For every run, the per-cell mean of Vmem over iterations 2000–2999, long after release. The tissue never settles and cycles on a scale of roughly 1000–1500 iterations (§12.2), so a late 1000-iteration average reduces, but does not remove, dependence on where in that cycle a run happens to be. Part 2 also records Vmem and G_pol at iteration 99, the last held iteration.
Face outlines
Dashed boxes mark the 14 target feature cells: two 2 × 2 eyes, a three-cell nose and a three-cell mouth.

Part 1 · trained seeds

Seeds
All 96 Gpol-only, depth-1 checkpoints: 12 seeds for each of four training objectives (correlation and globalsum, §12.4; facialFeatureOnly, §12.10; facialFeatureBalanced, §12.11) at each of two hold durations, 100 or 301 iterations. Each code is two-fold mirror symmetric and constant during its hold.
Dial
A seed's mean clamp value over its 40 ring cells.
Dial groups
Seeds ranked by dial: the lowest 24 (ranks 1–24), middle 24 (ranks 37–60) and highest 24 (ranks 73–96). A group's pattern is the per-cell mean of its members' readouts.
Change map
Each readout is first centred on its own spatial mean. The map is the highest-24 mean minus the lowest-24 mean. The group-to-group distances (1.8 and 3.6 mV) are RMS differences between centred group means.
Pattern PC2
Readouts are centred on their own spatial mean, and each cell is standardised across the 96 seeds before PCA. PC2's sign is set so its score rises with the dial. The r = +0.79 compares the change map with the PC2 loading.
Lobes
The 15 cells with the most negative and the 15 with the most positive PC2 loadings. A lobe's value is its cells' mean centred Vmem, averaged over the lowest or highest 24 seeds.
Dial–mode correlation
ρ between dial and PC2 score. For the configuration-controlled version, both variables are ranked separately within each of the 8 hold × objective cells (12 seeds each), and the pooled ranks are then correlated. The in-range figures repeat both using only the 83 seeds whose dial is ≥ 0; PCA is not refitted.
Code PC1
PCA of the 40 ring values across the 96 seeds. Each cell is centred across seeds but not standardised, and each code's own mean is kept so the dial stays in. Harmonic shares are the squared magnitudes of the discrete Fourier coefficients of the loading, walking clockwise from the top-left corner, as a share of orders 0–20; orders 0–8 are shown.

Part 2 · dial sweep on model 1888

Reference model
Checkpoint 1888 (Gpol-only D1, hold 100, trained on facialFeatureBalanced). Of the 96, it has the most dial-like trained code, with 90.6% of its code power in order 0 and all values between 0.003 and 0.94. Its trained code is replaced; its parameters, initial state, 100-iteration hold and run length are all kept.
Dial sweep
Every ring cell held at the same value, 0, 0.01, …, 2: 201 runs.
Step size and jumps
A step's size is the RMS over all 121 cells of the difference between the readouts at consecutive dial levels. A jump (tipping point) is a step larger than 2 mV. The per-regime medians group steps by their midpoint. The "share of distance" is the jumps' summed size over all 200 steps' summed size.
Hyperpolarised
Vmem below −29.3 mV, the single-cell saddle. Ring cells are read at iteration 99; interior cells are the 81 cells inside the ring and are read both at iteration 99 and from the late readout.
In window
An interior cell whose mean G_pol/G_ref over iterations 2000–2999 lies in [0.802, 1.439].
Contrast
Standard deviation of the late readout across the 81 interior cells. Plateau averages cover dials 0–0.35, 0.36–1.10, 1.20–1.50 and 1.61–2.
Churn
Standard deviation of each interior cell's Vmem over iterations 2000–2999, averaged over the 81 cells.
Face parts
A cell is dark when its readout is below −34.6 mV. Coverage adds up each part's dark fraction (two eyes, nose, mouth; 0–4). Separated parts counts covered parts whose dark cells share no 4-connected dark interior region with another part (0–4). Spurious is the dark fraction of the 67 non-feature interior cells. Part score = coverage + separated parts − 2 × spurious, at most 8. The trained seeds' comparison scores use their own late readouts, the same measure.
Clamp-free reference
Checkpoint 1888 run for 3400 iterations with no clamp at all, from the same initial state, with Vmem and G_pol/G_ref kept at every iteration. Its late pattern is averaged over the same absolute iterations as each sweep's readout (2000–2999 for the fixed readout, 2201–3200 and 2400–3399 for the aligned 301- and 500-iteration holds). Its held-state counterpart is its Vmem at iteration 99, 300 or 499. Every free pattern keeps all its energy under the square's 8 symmetries.
Distance from no clamp
RMS over all 121 cells (or the 81 interior cells, where stated) of a sweep's pattern minus the free pattern read over the same iterations. A dial "leaves no trace" when this is below 1 mV. The free ring's own conductance is the mean G_pol/G_ref of its 40 cells over the hold's iterations in the free run.
Release-aligned readout
The 301- and 500-iteration sweeps were repeated with the run lengthened by 201 and 400 iterations (3201 and 3400 in all), so the late readout covers iterations 2201–3200 and 2400–3399: 1900–2899 iterations after release, as with the 100-iteration hold. A lengthened run is identical to the original up to iteration 2999; this was checked at dial 1.0 to machine precision, for the state at the end of the hold and for the mean over iterations 2000–2999. Jumps "reappear" when a jump midpoint lies within 0.015 of one in the other sweep.
Longer holds
The same 201 dials with the ring held for iterations 0–300 or 0–499 instead of 0–99; the late readout stays at iterations 2000–2999. The 96 checkpoints differ only in their clamp code, hold length and training records, so the 301-iteration sweep is the uniform-dial sweep of every hold-301 checkpoint; no checkpoint was trained with a 500-iteration hold. End-of-hold readouts are then taken at iteration 300 or 499. The same-dial difference is the RMS over 121 cells between the two holds' late readouts at one dial. A long-hold pattern's nearest match is its smallest RMS distance to any of the 201 short-hold late readouts, and the quoted values are medians over dials 0–0.80, 0.81–1.44 and 1.45–2. Jumps "reappear at the same dial" when their midpoints coincide at the sweep's 0.01 resolution.

Correction · the two gradient runs

Codes
Each ring cell holds DC + G·cos(θ − φ), where θ is the cell's angle about the lattice centre, measured clockwise from straight up (toward row 0). Each gradient run is paired with a G = 0 twin at the same DC. Its differential is the gradient run's readout minus the twin's.
Earlier run
150 pairs with DC uniform on [−0.10, 0.65], G log-uniform on [0.01, 0.5] and φ uniform on [0, 2π), random seed 1. Values were clipped to [−1, 1], a clip no run reached. The rotation grid held DC at 0.381 with G in {0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4} × 8 directions.
In-range run
150 pairs with DC uniform on [0.01, 1.99], G log-uniform on [0.01, min(0.5, DC, 2 − DC)] and φ uniform on [0, 2π), random seed 1. Every held value lies in [0, 2]. The rotation grid is the same, except that the top strength is 0.38.
Reproducible by a dial
For each pair that moved more than 1 mV RMS: 1 − d² / s², where s is the differential's RMS and d is the RMS distance from the gradient run's readout to the nearest point on the piecewise-linear curve through all 150 twins, ordered by DC.
Symmetric share
A map's energy after averaging it over the 8 symmetries of the square, divided by its original energy; median over the pairs that moved more than 1 mV.
Turns with the gradient
For each G of the rotation grid, the 8 differentials are split into circular harmonics of φ. The quoted share is the first harmonic's fraction of their total energy.
Dimensions
Participation ratio (Σλ)² / Σλ² of the PCA eigenvalues of the 150 differentials.
Predictable modes
PCA of the differentials, 8 components. Each mode's score is predicted from DC, log G, G·cos φ, G·sin φ, cos φ, sin φ and DC·G by RBF kernel ridge regression, scored as 6-fold cross-validated R², with regularisation and kernel width chosen by an inner 3-fold grid search. The whole-map R² reconstructs all differentials from the predicted scores.
Reach by shell
Shells are concentric square rings, from shell 0 (the boundary) to shell 5 (the centre cell). The value is the dial effect's RMS over each shell's cells.
Effect vs dial
ρ between differential size and DC after ranking both and removing the linear dependence of each rank on G's rank. Band medians use pairs with G between 0.01 and 0.05.

Part 4 · harmonics

Shells
Concentric square rings of cells: shell 0 is the 40-cell boundary ring, then 32, 24, 16 and 8 cells, plus the centre cell. Each shell is read clockwise from its top-left corner, the same order used for the clamp code.
Circular orders
For a shell with n cells, c_k = (1/n) Σ_j x_j e^(−2πikj/n). Energy in order k is n|c_k|², folding k and n − k together, and the centre cell counts as order 0. For fully symmetric maps, c_k is real and nonzero only for k = 0, 4, 8, …. The signed amplitude is 2c_k (c_k for k = 0 or n/2). A negative order-4 amplitude means the corners are more hyperpolarised than the edge midpoints: an X.
Symmetry of the sweeps
Every late pattern in both dial sweeps keeps all its energy after averaging over the 8 symmetries of the square (symmetric share 1.000000). The 21 amplitudes therefore describe each pattern exactly.
X↔plus switches
Per shell, a dial counts as X when the order-4 amplitude is below −2 mV, plus when above +2 mV, and neutral otherwise. A switch is a change between X and plus, ignoring neutral dials in between. It is "between neighbouring dials" when the two are at most 0.02 apart. Regions are assigned by the dial at which the new motif appears: below 1.44, the ring-flip zone 1.44–1.62, and above 1.62, with 0–0.80 and 0–1.30 also counted on their own. The 4 mV counts repeat this with ±4 mV.
Opposite nearby motifs
All dial pairs at most 0.2 apart (at most 20 steps). A pair counts when, on some shell from 1 to 4, both order-4 amplitudes exceed the threshold with opposite signs. The "below 1.44" figures use pairs whose higher dial is below 1.44.
Movement at jumps
For each amplitude, the summed absolute change across the jump steps (above 2 mV RMS) over its summed absolute change across all 200 steps. Quoted for the ten amplitudes with the largest standard deviation across dials.
Lattice cosine modes
(p, q) = cos(πp(r + ½)/11) cos(πq(c + ½)/11), normalised: the 2D DCT-II basis. These are eigenvectors of the 4-neighbour lattice Laplacian with no wrap-around (residual 4 × 10⁻¹⁵). The model's gap-junction current is that Laplacian weighted by voltage-gated conductances. The fully symmetric modes are (p, q) + (q, p) with p and q even, 21 in all. Spatial frequency is √(p² + q²), and "above 4" excludes modes at or below 4.
Axial–diagonal index
(E_axial − E_diagonal) / (E_axial + E_diagonal) of a pattern's DCT power, excluding (0, 0). Axial modes have a wavevector angle atan(q/p) within 22.5° of 0° or 90°; diagonal modes are within 22.5° of 45°. Templates with dark diagonals or dark centre row and column give −0.95 and +0.94. Switches use ±0.2 with the same neutral-zone rule. Mode-by-mode sign switches use the 8 symmetric modes that vary most, with coefficients in mV RMS and thresholds of 1 and 2 mV.
Nearby correlation
Pearson correlation between two late patterns after subtracting each one's mean, for dial pairs at most 0.2 apart with the higher dial below 1.44. This uses no basis.
Order classes
Odd orders; orders 2, 6, 10, …; orders 0, 4, 8, …. These are the square's symmetry types: E; B1 + B2; A1 + A2. In cosine modes they are (odd, even) and (even, odd) modes; the transpose-antisymmetric part of (even, even) plus the transpose-symmetric part of (odd, odd); and the rest. Energies agree between the two bases to 10⁻¹⁵.
Overall progression
Each sweep's 21 amplitudes (shell means and orders 4, 8, … on shells 0–4, plus the centre) are read along the dial, in six regions: all dials, 0–0.80 (below the lower edge of the single-cell bistable window, 0.802), 0–1.30, below 1.44, 1.44–1.62 and above 1.62. Per amplitude, four scale-free measures are used: the share of its total movement in single 0.01 steps larger than a tenth of its range; total variation over range (1 = one-way); largest step over range; and the share of its variance (DCT along the dial) at periods of at least 0.4, omitted for the 1.44–1.62 region. The cells count the amplitudes on which each hold is smoother and give a Wilcoxon signed-rank p across the 21. For the pattern as a whole, the trajectory is weighted either by energy, so distances equal RMS over cells (quoted in mV RMS), or with each amplitude divided by its standard deviation over the two sweeps being compared. From it come path length, straightness (end-to-end distance over path length) and the share of the path in steps above the 90th percentile of all steps from those two sweeps. The five-sweep figure puts every sweep on one footing, with the scale and large-step threshold pooled over all five. The trajectory map uses the equally weighted amplitudes, centred and projected on their two leading principal components computed from all three sweeps together. These measures were fixed after the 100- and 301-iteration comparison was first explored and before the 500-iteration hold was simulated, so the 500-iteration results test them. The exploratory measures mentioned in the caption came before them.
Gradient transfer
For each of Part 3's 450 changes (gradient run minus twin), the class shares of its energy over all 121 cells, binned by distance to the nearest jump (below 0.025, 0.025–0.1, 0.1 or more) and by G (0.01–0.05, 0.05–0.5). Bars show the median shares, rescaled to sum to 1. The odd-order split uses the 115 runs at least 0.1 from a jump with G below 0.05.
Trained seeds
Code: the 40 ring clamp values, with class shares of the energy outside order 0. Pattern: the late readout, with class shares of the energy outside order 0 summed over shells. ρ compares the same class across the 96 seeds.
