The Stripes

The patterns

The target, the tissue with no clamp, and the best ring code of each size

Finding 1

A ring that may cross the bistable edge writes the stripes with two harmonics

The window is thin, and tied to the 1.439 edge

Mapping the ring codes a₀ + a₂ cos 2θ (the oval) shows where the stripe forms. Written in the ring's level at the top and bottom, T = a₀ + a₂, and at the sides, S = a₀ − a₂, the window is a thin slice in T that starts at the bistable edge, and the sides' level decides how completely the stripe's ends close.

Finding 2

The stripe is written by the two ends of the ring during the hold, and left standing when the flanks relight

It is not the face's kind of pattern. The face was latched on a second rise of the conductance, hundreds of iterations after the release, and no part of the ring wrote it alone. The stripe is written by two stretches of the ring, in the hold, and is scored before the interior's conductance has turned.

Which part of the ring writes it

The model was run with only part of the ring held to the stripe code's values for the hold, the rest left to its own rules, and read at the stripe code's best moment against the same run with no ring held. The six cells above and below the centre stripe are the only ones the code holds above the 1.439 edge of a cell's bistable window (outlined), and with the rest of the top and bottom rows they write all of it.

The two ends are written independently

When it is written, and when it is left standing

All 27 stripe cells are already dark by the last iteration of the hold, held up by conductance the ring's push has lifted. After the release the interior's conductance collapses; the flanks fall through the bottom of their switching window first and relight, and what is left is the stripe, at iteration 504, well before the interior's conductance has turned.

How the push reaches the stripe

The relay is an exact decomposition of the difference between the stripe code's run and a baseline that has the clamp's tissue-wide update but never holds the ring: at every step, how much of the final selectivity gap each cell is carrying, and which cell handed it on. The movie draws it over the ten named blocks: the ring's four sides, each flank in two halves, the centre stripe in two halves.

The story so far

Two springs, a flood and an ebb

Two springs. Think of every interior cell as a basin that is either dry or flooded and tips when its water level, its conductance, crosses a high mark or a low mark. The ring is a line of taps. For 300 iterations the taps run, and the ten at the middle of the top and bottom edges, the two ends of the stripe, are opened past the tipping point itself (above 1.439) so those basins can only be flooded. Through the tissue's shared field they push water into the two ends of the middle channel.

A flood. By iteration 300 the whole channel, all 27 cells, is flooded, and so are 14 flank cells next to it, which sit lower in the water.

An ebb. The taps shut and everything starts to drain, but not alike: the channel was filled highest and keeps its water longest; the flank cells, filled lowest, fall through their low mark first and go dry, all 14 of them. For 34 iterations, 479 to 512, the only water left is the channel: the stripe. Then it drains too. The ebb is the tissue's own, a collapse of its conductance that happens whether or not the taps are shut; with the ring held for the whole run the stripe still appears, at iteration 472.

What the picture does not claim. Nothing is drawn cell by cell and nothing travels along the stripe as a wave. The sides of the ring are not idle (they give 19% of the whole effect on their own) but they are not what writes the stripe. The stripe is not the face's second rise: the water never comes back before it is read. The window is thin: a ring held a few hundredths too low or too high does not make it.

Methods

Terms, and how each one is measured

The stripes
The interior's nine columns cut into three stripes of three columns, each nine rows tall (rows 1 to 9). The centre stripe (columns 4 to 6) is the target's hyperpolarised region, −60 mV, 27 cells. The two flanks (columns 1 to 3 and 7 to 9) and the ring are held at −5 mV in the target. The design document's pattern 6.
Ring, interior
The tissue is 121 cells in an 11 × 11 square lattice, each gap-junction coupled to its four nearest neighbours, with no wrap-around. The 40 cells on the outer edge are the ring; the other 81 are the interior. No ring cell is part of any stripe: the ring carries the code and, as for the face, is scored at the background value.
Hold, release
For iterations 0 to 300 every ring cell's polarising conductance G_pol is overwritten, every iteration, with its value in the code. From iteration 301 the ring follows the model's own rules.
Ring code, order
The code is a₀ + a₁ cos θ + … + a_N cos Nθ over a ring cell's angle θ from straight up, in G_pol / G_ref (ratio to 1 nS). Order n is the harmonic cos nθ. Every code is left-right symmetric; the stripes are also top-bottom symmetric, so the even orders alone span the codes with both symmetries. "Orders 0–N" is every order up to N; "even orders" only 0, 2, 4, …
Two routes
The first route is the two-harmonic code of ceiling 2.0, which holds ten ring cells above 1.439 and writes the stripe in the hold. The second route (exploratory) is the even-only code of every order up to 20 at the face's ceiling, which never exceeds 1.3, writes part of the stripe in the hold and reads it late, after the first trough of the interior's conductance. Even-only arm: a set of restarts that search only the even orders listed (the arm 0, 2, 4, 6; the arm 0, …, 10; the arm 0, …, 20). Rung 2′: the three even-only arms at ceiling 1.3. Second peak: the highest interior mean conductance after the trough over the whole replay; low-order residual: the root-mean-square difference between a code's 40 ring values and their least-squares fit by orders 0, 2, 4 and 6.
Ceiling
The largest value a ring cell may be held at. The face used 1.3. A lone cell is forced hyperpolarised above 1.439, so a ceiling of 2.0, the conductance equation's own limit, lets a held ring cell do that.
Balanced RMS
The score. The mean of two root-mean-square errors against the target: over the 27 centre-stripe cells and over the other 94 cells (the 54 flank cells and the 40 ring cells). It is computed at every iteration from the release to 2,999 and a code's score is its lowest value; the iteration where it occurs is the code's best moment. The weights on the stripe, the flanks and the ring are 0.50, 0.29 and 0.21, almost the face's 0.50, 0.31, 0.19.
Overlap
Of the interior cells that are hyperpolarised (Vmem below −34.6 mV) or in the centre stripe, the share that are both: the structural intersection over union, 1 for the stripe and nothing else, 0 for none of it. Read at a code's best moment. The stripes count as formed at 0.9 or more.
Restart, rung, arm
A restart is one run of the search from one starting code, half from the library of earlier codes and half from a random allowed code. An arm is the restarts of one code size; a rung is a set of arms run together under one registration. The rungs: 1, the pilot at ceiling 1.3 (population 16, 20 restarts of each of orders 0 to 3 and of the even orders 0 and 2); 2, the full run at 1.3 (population 64, 100 generations, 10 restarts of orders 3 to 6 and of the even sets {0, 2, 4} and {0, 2, 4, 6}); 2′ (exploratory, Amendment 6), the even-only sets {0, 2, 4, 6} (32 restarts), {0, …, 10} and {0, …, 20} (16 each) at 1.3; 3 and 3b, the same pilot and full run at ceiling 2.0, the ceiling at which the first stripe was found; 4′ (exploratory, stopped), 16 restarts of order 4 at 2.0, population 16. What each was registered to test is in the registration and its amendments, committed before the runs.
Bistable window, edge
A lone cell, with no neighbours, has two stable voltages when its G_pol / G_ref lies between 0.802 and 1.439: a depolarised one near −10 mV and a hyperpolarised one near −50 mV. Below 0.802 only the first exists; above 1.439 only the second. 1.439 is the window's upper edge. Neighbours move both marks by about 0.035 each (the switch rule, below).
Dark, light, strays
A cell is dark when its Vmem is below −34.6 mV, midway between the target's −60 and the background's −9. Strays are dark interior cells outside the centre stripe.
Selectivity
The mean G_pol / G_ref over the 27 stripe cells minus the mean over the 54 flank cells. The selectivity difference of a run is its selectivity minus that of the baseline run with no ring held.
Best moment, trough, peaks
The best moment is the iteration at which a code's balanced RMS is lowest after the release (iteration 504 for the stripe code). The interior mean is the mean G_pol / G_ref over the 81 interior cells. Its first peak is its maximum inside the hold, its trough its minimum between iterations 302 and 1,300, and its second peak its maximum after the trough.
Switch rule
Each cell is a switch with a memory: given its neighbours, two conductances G_down and G_up bound the window in which both of its voltages exist; above G_up it is forced dark, below G_down forced light, between them it keeps whichever it is on. Each light neighbour raises both marks by about 0.036 and each dark one lowers them by about 0.032. The rule's one-step accuracy is the share of cell-moments at which, given the true state, it names the cell's next state. Script: analyzeBoundaryHarmonicSwitchRule11x11.py, from a replay of every code's run (recordBoundaryHarmonicRuns11x11.py) and each cell's stable voltages (computeBoundaryHarmonicBranches11x11.py).
Relay, baseline, transfer
An exact decomposition (computeBoundaryHarmonicRelay11x11.py, through boundaryHarmonicStep.py, a pure re-implementation of one model step checked against the model) of the stripe code's run minus a baseline run into the network that carries the difference. The clamp does two things on every iteration of the hold: it writes the ring's conductances, and it re-solves the voltage of all 121 cells once more. The baseline has the second but not the first (extraUpdateOnly), so the difference is what holding the ring adds. At every step the difference at the best moment is split exactly into what each cell holds of it and what each cell hands to which, along two channels: the field (the start-of-step voltage entering through the extracellular field) and the gap junctions (through the contact current and the cell's own membrane). A net transfer is what one block hands another minus what it receives back, summed over a phase's windows. Summing cells into blocks cancels opposite transfers between them and drops transfers inside a block, so a coarse arrow is a net and can differ in direction and size from the strongest cell-to-cell arrows. The relay is read at the best moment, 504, with no second rise assumed.
Blocks, phases
Ten named blocks: the ring's top (with its corners), bottom, left and right sides; the left and right flanks, each cut into an upper and a lower half; the centre stripe, cut into an upper and a lower half. The cut is through the lattice's middle row (row 5), whose cells carry half their weight in each half. The left and right blocks are mirror images and only the left and centre ones are read. The relay is stored in windows of 50 iterations. Flood is the windows inside the hold (iterations 0–299), clear the windows from the release to the best moment; there is no write phase because the stripe is read before the interior's conductance has turned.
Ring segments
Hold only some ring cells at the stripe code's values for the hold (with the clamp's tissue-wide update as ever) and compare with the baseline, read at the best moment (analyzeBoundaryHarmonicStripeRingSegments11x11.py). The top and bottom rows are the 22 cells of rows 0 and 10; the left and right columns the 18 cells of columns 0 and 10 between them; the six stripe-facing cells are columns 4–6 of the top and bottom rows. The controls are 200 random subsets of the same size drawn from the ring (seed 20261001); a set beats them when its selectivity difference exceeds their 95th percentile.
T, S, the oval plane
A code of orders 0 to 2 with no tilt, a₀ + a₂ cos 2θ, holds the ring at T = a₀ + a₂ at the top and bottom and at S = a₀ − a₂ at the sides. The landscape maps (simulateBoundaryHarmonicStripeLandscape11x11.py) replay a grid of such codes and read each at its best moment.
δ, ε, the end cells
The end cells are the ten ring cells the stripe code holds above 1.439: the middle five of the top row and the middle five of the bottom row. δ is a shift added to them and ε a shift added to the other thirty ring cells, each clipped to the ceiling (simulateBoundaryHarmonicStripeEdgeScan11x11.py).
Registered prediction, amendment, gate
Each analysis's criteria were written to a JSON file and committed before the analysis was run: the registration of the training, five amendments to it (each committed before the runs it changes, saying what had been seen), and the registration of the mechanism analyses. The gate was G1, at least one restart at overlap 0.9 or more at its best moment, and G2, that code's score at least 5 mV below the best of 64 random allowed codes of its size. The verdicts in the appendix apply each criterion as written, and where an amendment fixes how it is read, as it fixes it.
The words of the storyline
Taps are the 40 ring cells; springs the ten end cells; a basin an interior cell; its water level its conductance; flooded dark and dry light; the flood the hold, in which the ring pushes the interior's conductance up; the ebb the collapse of the interior's conductance after the first peak, the tissue's own feedback running, which happens whether or not the ring is released.
Slider, grid, offset
The relay loop and the pattern sliders move the ring code's coefficients one or two at a time away from the stripe code (a0 1.350, a1 0.001, a2 0.137); the other orders stay at their trained values. An offset is the amount added to a coefficient: a multiple of the trained coefficient would do nothing to a1 and would step over the stripe's window of a few thousandths. The relay slider has 15 or 16 stops per order, closely spaced near the trained value (offsets 0, ±0.01, ±0.02, ±0.04, ±0.08, ±0.15, ±0.3, ±0.6) and including the order's knockout (coefficient 0), and the grids have five offsets on each axis (−0.30, −0.08, 0, +0.08, +0.30); every stop is a full relay run, read at iteration 504. Ring values are clipped to 0–2 G_pol / G_ref, the range the conductance can take. The pattern sliders are denser (201 stops over the allowed range, 121 over ±0.06) and need only the replay, not the relay. All of it is exploratory: nothing in it was registered.
Steering lab
An estimate, not a model: for a setting of the three orders, the ring's four region levels (top, upper sides, lower sides, bottom) are computed exactly, and the network shown is a Gaussian-kernel average, over region-level space, of the edges that the nearest simulated codes carry among their three biggest transfers of a phase. The simulated codes are the page's own and a sweep of 400 (Sobol points over the allowed coefficients, a cloud around the stripe code, and codes spread over the window); the kernel width is chosen by holding out whole blocks of the space (8 k-means blocks) and the held-out skill is reported beside it. Script: assembleRelayLoopStripesSteeringData11x11.py.
Procedures

Summary and controls: analyzeBoundaryHarmonicTraining11x11.py (each size's best code replayed for 20,000 iterations, 32 nearby codes, 64 random allowed codes, the tissue with no clamp, the best stored sweep code). Replays: analyzeBoundaryHarmonicFieldRole11x11.py, the stripe code with the field's transduction on or off and the ring released after the hold or held throughout. Scoring: analyzeBoundaryHarmonicStripeTrainingPredictions11x11.py, analyzeBoundaryHarmonicStripeProgram11x11.py, analyzeBoundaryHarmonicStripeRelay11x11.py. Movie: buildBoundaryHarmonicRelayVariantMovie11x11.py; relay loop, slider, grid, steering lab: buildBoundaryHarmonicRingCodeStripeLevels11x11.py and ...StripeSweep11x11.py (the codes), runBoundaryHarmonicStripeRelayLoopRelays.sh (one relay per code, with extractRelayLoopSweepRecord11x11.py --withTrajectory), assembleRelayLoopStripesData11x11.py and assembleRelayLoopStripesSteeringData11x11.py; pattern sliders: simulateBoundaryHarmonicRingCodePatterns11x11.py; the page's script is a port of the face's, tested on this data.
