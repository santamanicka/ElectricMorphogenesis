The Pinched Dial

The Tilted Dial added order 1 to the boundary dial: a gradient that raises one side of the ring. This report adds order 2, which raises two opposite sides and lowers the two sides between them, pinching the ring's values into an oval. First order 2 on its own, then orders 1 and 2 together. Same model, hold, readout and ceiling as before.

Order 2 on its own

The patterns

The tissue is an 11 × 11 lattice of cells. Its ring cells are the 40 cells on the outer edge. During the hold (the first 301 iterations) each ring cell's polarising conductance is fixed at a chosen value. The dial (DC) gives every ring cell the same value. The second order (strength G2) adds to two opposite sides of the ring and takes the same amount off the two sides between them. Every held value stays between 0 and 1.3. Each thumbnail below shows what the interior settles into long after the hold. Dark cells are hyperpolarised. All terms are defined under Methods.

Both orders together

Now the tilt (order 1, strength G1, raising the top of the ring) and the oval (order 2, strength G2) are combined. Both are kept mirror-symmetric about the vertical axis, as the target face is. With G2 positive the oval's long axis is vertical, raising top and bottom; with G2 negative it is horizontal, raising the sides. Each panel fixes the dial, and moves G1 up the rows and G2 across the columns. The middle column is the tilt alone and the bottom row the oval alone.

The code

Each ring cell is held at DC + G1·cos(θ − φ1) + G2·cos 2(θ − φ2), with θ the cell's angle about the lattice centre, measured clockwise from straight up. The order-1 term raises the side facing φ1. The order-2 term raises the sides facing φ2 and φ2 + 180° and lowers the two sides at right angles to them. On its own, order 2 is run at φ2 = 0° (raising top and bottom), 45° (raising the top-right and bottom-left corners) and 22.5° between; by the square's symmetries these cover every direction. Together with order 1, both harmonics point straight up (φ1 = φ2 = 0°) and G2 takes either sign.

Finding 1

On its own, the oval reaches fewer patterns than the tilt, and pinched toward the corners it acts as a switch

As with the tilt, the oval does little until some ring cells are pushed past one of the dial's jumps (0.695, 0.775, 1.195 and 1.245–1.265). Of the 392 codes per direction whose ring stays clear of every jump, none changes by more than 2 mV at 0°, 5 at 22.5° and 49 at 45°. How it acts past a jump depends on its direction. Finding 2

Together, the two orders simply add up until a jump is reached; past it they interact, and that is where new patterns come from

If the tilt and the oval acted independently, the pattern with both would be the dial alone plus the tilt's change plus the oval's change. The figure below makes that prediction for two codes and sets it beside what the tissue does.

The extra inflection points are not what matters

You asked that the second order add inflection points to the code, so every claim above about both orders holds for the 3,118 codes above the threshold. But is the threshold itself special? Two tests say no.

The storyline

The story so far, in plain terms

The setup. Picture the 40 edge cells as a ring of dimmer switches around a room. The dial turns them all together. The tilt (order 1) turns one side up and the other down, like tilting a tray. The oval (order 2) pinches the ring: two opposite sides up and the two between down, like squeezing a rubber band into an oval.

The oval on its own. Like the tilt, it only matters once some switches are pushed past one of the dial's clicks. It reaches fewer new patterns than the tilt. Pinched toward two corners it behaves like a toggle: past a certain squeeze the tissue snaps into one arrangement, a dark diagonal line, and squeezing harder mostly keeps it there. It also has a few clicks of its own that the dial never had.

Tilt and oval together. Think of two musicians. Playing softly, each stays out of the other's way, and what you hear is simply both parts added together. Once either plays loud enough to push part of the ring past a click, they change each other's effect, and chords come out that neither plays alone: 142 new motifs on top of the 77 either order makes by itself.

What does not matter. Whether the ring's profile gains an extra bend (an inflection point) makes no difference by itself. What counts is how far each edge cell's value is pushed relative to the dial's clicks.

Appendix · supporting analyses

A1 · The dial alone, below 1.3

With the 301-iteration hold, turning the dial from 0 to 1.3 moves the pattern mostly in six jumps: at 0.695 (6.3 mV RMS between dials 0.01 apart), 0.775 (2.0 mV), 1.195 (2.1 mV) and a cluster at 1.245–1.265 (up to 6.5 mV). Every pattern keeps the same five dark cells. This is the baseline both orders are measured against; The Tilted Dial describes it in full.

A2 · The oval's landscape, point by point

Every order-2 code on its own, with the same measures as in The Tilted Dial. The dashed lines mark where the highest (rising to the left) or lowest (rising to the right) ring cell reaches a dial jump. Point at a code for its ring, pattern, the dial alone at the same DC, and the difference; click to keep it.

A3 · The seams, cell by cell

As in The Tilted Dial: every ring cell has its own seams, where its held value reaches a dial jump, and one grid step (0.02 in DC or G2) carries some ring cells across a jump, or none.

A4 · Before a seam the oval's change keeps its own symmetry; past one it tips

An order-2 clamp drives circular orders 2, 6, 10, …: the part of a change that is unchanged by a half turn but flips sign under a quarter turn. The dial moves orders 0, 4, 8, …; orders 1, 3, 5, … are neither.

A5 · The gain in variety does not hinge on the threshold

Binarising a time-averaged pattern is sensitive to cells near the threshold, so motifs were counted at four thresholds. Spread was also measured without any threshold, as effective dimensions.

A6 · Checks, and three codes that break their own symmetry

The 512 single-order codes run in both the combined grid and the single-order grids agree to within 3 × 10⁻¹¹ mV. Order 2's symmetry checks (codes at 90°, 180°, −22.5° and 67.5° against rotated or mirrored grid runs) agree to within 0.0004 mV. Combined codes run with the tilt reversed match the upside-down image of the original to within 0.0003 mV. Every combined code's ring is exactly left–right symmetric, and so is the tissue at the end of the hold. Nearly every late pattern keeps that symmetry too, but three do not.

Methods

Terms

Lattice, cell
The tissue is an 11 × 11 square lattice of 121 cells, indexed row by row from the top-left (row 0 at the top). Each cell is coupled by gap junctions to its four nearest neighbours; the lattice does not wrap around at its edges.
Ring cells, edge cells
The 40 cells on the lattice's outer edge: all of row 0, row 10, column 0 and column 10. These are the only cells the clamp acts on. The storyline calls them edge cells.
Interior cells
The other 81 cells, rows and columns 1 to 9. Motifs are read from these.
Vmem
A cell's membrane voltage, in millivolts (mV). Lower is more hyperpolarised and drawn darker.
G_pol, G_ref
G_pol is the conductance of a cell's polarising ion channel, the channel that pulls Vmem down. G_ref = 1 nS. Held values are G_pol / G_ref; the model keeps G_pol within [0, 2 G_ref], and this report keeps held values in [0, 1.3].
Hold, release
During the hold, iterations 0 to 300, each ring cell's G_pol is overwritten on every iteration with its held value × G_ref. From iteration 301 on (release), the ring cells follow the model's own dynamics.
Code
The 40 held values, DC + G1·cos(θ − φ1) + G2·cos 2(θ − φ2).
Dial (DC)
The order-0 part of the code: one value shared by all 40 ring cells. "DC" is borrowed from electronics, where it means "direct current", the constant part of a signal. Here it is the ring's average held value.
Tilt, order 1 (G1, φ1)
G1·cos(θ − φ1): one cosine around the ring, highest on the side facing φ1. θ is a ring cell's angle about the lattice centre, measured clockwise from straight up (toward row 0).
Oval, order 2 (G2, φ2)
G2·cos 2(θ − φ2): two cycles around the ring, highest on the two sides facing φ2 and φ2 + 180°, lowest on the two sides at right angles to them. φ2 = 0° raises top and bottom; 45° raises the top-right and bottom-left corners. With both orders, φ1 = φ2 = 0° and G2 takes either sign: positive raises top and bottom, negative raises the left and right sides.
Circular order
Values around a closed loop decomposed into cosines and sines: order 0 is the mean, order k has k cycles around the loop.
Turning point, inflection point
Following the held values once around the ring, a turning point is a local high or low, and an inflection point is where the profile switches from bending one way to bending the other. The tilt alone has two of each; the oval alone has four of each.
Inflection threshold
With both orders, the ring's profile has four turning points and four inflection points exactly when |G2| > G1/4 (checked numerically on every code), and two of each otherwise. "Above the threshold" means the oval adds inflection points.
Ring range
A code's lowest to highest held value.
Late pattern
Each cell's mean Vmem over iterations 2000 to 2999 of a 3000-iteration run: the readout for every result and thumbnail here.
No clamp
The same model and initial state run with no hold, read over the same iterations.
Dial alone, dial sweep
Codes with no harmonic, run at every dial value from 0 to 2 in steps of 0.01 with the same hold and readout; only 0 to 1.3 is used.
RMS, change, Δ
The RMS (root-mean-square) difference between two patterns is the square root of the mean, over the 121 cells, of the squared difference in Vmem. A code's change, Δ, is its RMS difference from the dial alone at the same DC.
Step
The RMS difference between codes one grid step apart along one of the code's numbers. "Movement" is the sum of step sizes.
Jump, click
A step of more than 2 mV RMS between dial values 0.01 apart in the dial sweep, placed at the midpoint: 0.695, 0.775, 1.195, 1.245, 1.255 and 1.265 below 1.3. The storyline calls them clicks.
Spans a jump; crosses a jump; seam
A code spans a jump when the jump lies within its ring range. Between two codes, a ring cell crosses a jump when its held value lies on opposite sides of it in the two codes. A seam is the set of codes at which one ring cell's held value equals a jump.
Quiet region, wedge, ceiling band
Regions of the order-2 triangle by ring range: no jump; the jump at 0.695 or 0.775 but a top below 1.195; a top reaching 1.195–1.265.
Symmetry classes
A change split into its part that flips sign under a half turn (circular orders 1, 3, 5, …), its part unchanged by a half turn but flipping under a quarter turn (orders 2, 6, 10, …; what an order-2 clamp drives directly), and its part unchanged by a quarter turn (orders 0, 4, 8, …; "dial-like"). Shares are of summed squares.
Adding up, interaction share
For a code with both orders, the prediction is the dial alone plus the tilt alone's change plus the oval alone's change, at the same DC, G1 and G2. The interaction share is the summed squared difference between the actual change and that prediction, as a share of the actual change's summed squares.
New side
A combined code puts a ring cell on a new side of a jump when that cell's held value is on a different side of some jump than under either single order at the same numbers.
Off the track; off every single order
RMS distance to the nearest dial-alone pattern (0 to 1.3, steps of 0.01); and to the nearest pattern made by the dial alone, the tilt alone (step 0.02, φ1 = 0°) or the oval alone (step 0.02, φ2 = 0°, with negative G2 as quarter turns of positive).
Effective dimensions
The participation ratio (Σλ)² / Σλ² of the variances λ along the principal components of a set of patterns.
Motif; saddle
The set of interior cells whose late Vmem is below a threshold, −29.3 mV unless stated: the single-cell saddle, the unstable point between a lone cell's two resting states when its G_pol / G_ref lies between 0.802 and 1.439.
Storyline words
"Edge cells" for ring cells, "tilt" for order 1, "oval" and "pinch" for order 2, "clicks" for jumps.

Procedures

Model and simulation
Checkpoint 1888 (G_pol-only clamp on the ring, trained with a 100-iteration hold and the facialFeatureBalanced objective), with its learned code replaced. Tissue parameters are unchanged: gap-junction strength 0.05, and the bioelectric field enabled with screen size 4 and transduction weight 1000. So is the initial state, Vmem −9.2 mV on every cell. Time step 0.01, 3000 iterations, replayed with the call sequence of the Boundary Dial report.
Order-2 grid
DC from 0 to 1.30 and G2 from 0.02 to min(DC, 1.3 − DC), multiples of 0.02: 1,056 codes for each of φ2 = 0°, 22.5° and 45°. Sixteen symmetry checks were run at φ2 = 90°, 180°, −22.5° and 67.5°.
Combined grid
DC, G1 ≥ 0 and G2 of either sign, multiples of 0.04, every code whose held values all lie in [0, 1.3] (4,438, besides the dial alone). Four codes were also run with G1 reversed. Runs were split across 64 and 72 cluster jobs, and the results merged and checked for completeness.
Comparisons
Order-1 numbers are from The Tilted Dial's grid (same triangle, steps of 0.02). Motif counts for "either order alone" pool the dial alone, the tilt alone at φ1 = 0° and the oval alone at φ2 = 0° with both signs; the combined counts use the combined grid.
Caveat: churn
The tissue is still moving in the readout window: interior cells' Vmem standard deviation over iterations 2000–2999 averages about 5.7 mV. Patterns and motifs are time averages over that window.
