The Double Stripes

Can a ring code written onto the 40 edge cells of the 11 x 11 tissue make the two flank stripes dark while the centre stripe stays light, the inverse of the single stripe?

Not so far. 632 searches, 2.3 million simulated codes, both ceilings, contiguous and even-only codes up to order 20: none reaches the overlap of 0.9 that counts as formed. The best reaches 0.70, for an instant.

Opened 2026-10-02, updated 2026-10-03 Registered before the run: commit a9eef98, with four amendments Page built {{BUILT}} Companion: The Stripes (the single stripe)

Where the investigation stands

The registered ladder has been applied as written and has run out. Arms A, B and C, then rungs 1a, 1b, 2, 3a and 3b, each reported with its number of codes simulated. No stage reached the gate. The registration has no rung beyond order 0-20 at ceiling 2.0, and no further rung will be run (see the decision below).

Decision, 2026-10-03: no rung 4. The investigation is closed for now and stands as a negative result under the registered ladder. Three ways forward were considered and none was taken: a rung 4 that changes what the search can use (a ceiling above 2.0, sine terms, or local refinement of the best codes), and relaxing the target to what the codes do reach (narrower stripes). Nothing new was simulated or rescored for this page, and the registered scoring script has not been run.

Main findings

1. No ring code forms the double stripes, at either ceiling, by either route, at any size

  • Top-bottom symmetric codes are the most reliable. The even-only sets {0, 2, 4} and {0, 2, 4, 6} at ceiling 2.0 (rung 2) have 3 or 4 free values. Their median overlap is 0.58 and 47 of their 64 restarts reach 0.5 or more, against 12 of 16 for the contiguous orders 0-20 with 21 values. The best they reach is 0.65 and 0.69.
  • Eight restarts settled in a flooded interior. At ceiling 2.0, in the smallest codes (orders 0 and 0-1), the search ends with every interior cell dark. It is the highest overlap in arm B, but it is the opposite of two stripes with a light centre, so it is left out of every "best" on this page. Earlier progress notes quoted 0.67 for arm B; the best stripe-like pattern there is 0.62.
  • Random allowed codes do not come close. Across every code size the 64 random codes reach at most 0.49 (a plain uniform ring at ceiling 2.0). The trained codes beat them by about 0.2 in overlap and by 2 to 6 mV in the best score (A3).
  • A hand-designed ring does not form it either. The two-bump profiles proposed by the user (bumps on the top and bottom rows over the ends of the flank stripes) reach 0.56 for the pure form and 0.62 for the best form with a plateau (A5).

2. What the near-misses look like: ragged stripes about two columns wide, a lone centre cell, held only briefly

{{REACH_MAP}}

  • The cells two in from the ring are the core. Column 2 (and column 8) is dark in 95 to 99% of the good restarts in rows 2, 4, 5, 6 and 8, and in 58 to 77% in rows 1, 3, 7 and 9.

Storyline

Picture the tissue as a sheet of 121 small lamps with a frame of 40 dimmer switches around its edge. We set the 40 switches, hold them for a while, let go and look at which lamps end up dark. The target is two dark bands, one down the left third and one down the right third, with the middle third left light.

  1. We tried hard. 632 searches, with settings made from a single dial (every switch alike) up to 21 independent dials, and with the switches allowed up to 1.3 or up to 2.0. Not one gave the two bands.
  2. What the switches do paint is narrower and ragged. Two bands that are on average 1.7 lamps wide instead of 3, and one stray dark lamp at the centre of the sheet. The lamps of each band that face the middle are the ones most often left light.
  3. The best picture is a moment, not a state. It covers 40 of the 54 band lamps for an instant, is gone within a few hundred steps, and nudging the switches by a hundredth already makes it worse.
  4. A frame set by hand does not do better. Two bumps along the top and bottom, the user's idea, reach 0.56 to 0.62.

What the picture does not claim. That no setting exists: a search cannot show that, and the registered ladder stops at order 0-20 with a ceiling of 2.0 and cosine terms only. That the tissue cannot make the pattern some other way (a ceiling above 2.0, sine terms, a longer hold, clamping the interior and changing the tissue were not tried). Why the inner third of each band resists: no mechanism was analysed, since no code formed the pattern.

Appendix

A1. Design and registration

{{REGISTRATION_QUESTION}}

  • Tissue and hold. Model 1888, frozen. The 40 ring cells follow the ring code for the first 301 iterations (the hold), then follow the model's own rules (the release). The run is 3,000 iterations. The interior is never touched.
  • Score. Balanced root-mean-square error against the target: the mean of the error over the 54 flank cells and the error over the other 67 cells (27 centre-stripe cells and 40 ring cells). A code's score is its lowest value from iteration 301 to 2,999.
  • Formed. Overlap of at least 0.9, read at the code's best moment: of the interior cells that are dark or in the flanks, the share that are both.
  • Gate. G1: some restart is formed. G2: that code's score is at least 5 mV below the best of 64 random allowed codes of the same size. Every arm runs to completion whether or not another has met the gate.
  • Arms. A: ceiling 1.3, population 16, 150 generations, 20 restarts each of orders 0-0, 0-1, 0-2, 0-3 and of the even-only set {0, 2}. B: the same at ceiling 2.0. C: after A, ceiling 1.3, population 64, 100 generations, even-only sets {0, 2, 4, 6} (32 restarts), {0, 2, ..., 10} (16) and {0, 2, ..., 20} (16), seeded from arm A's folder.
  • The ladder, as applied. Registered as an instruction: if nothing forms, extend and continue. Rung 1: contiguous orders 3-6 at population 64, 10 restarts each, at both ceilings (1a, 1b; Amendment 2, d0680f3). Rung 2: even-only {0, 2, 4} and {0, 2, 4, 6} at ceiling 2.0, 32 restarts each (Amendment 3, 70d4939). Rung 3: contiguous orders 0-8 up to 0-20 in steps of 2 at ceiling 2.0, 16 restarts each, at population 16 (3a) and then 64 (3b), the second only because the first formed nothing (Amendment 4, f719e69). Order 0-20 at ceiling 2.0 spans all 21 independent mirror-symmetric ring values. Sine terms, which would break the mirror symmetry, are not allowed. A note, bbbe285, says how G2 and C1 are read for the even-only sets.
  • Which code is analysed. The qualifying code with the lowest score would be the primary code, and the best qualifying code of every distinct route would go to the mechanism stage. No code qualified, so there is no primary code and the mechanism stage was not run.
  • Controls. 64 random allowed codes per code size, the tissue with no clamp, and the best stored sweep code.
  • Replays of the primary code. Registered: 20,000 iterations with the ring released; the same with the field off; the ring held for all 3,000 iterations. The summary script's 20,000-iteration replay was run on the best code of every size it summarised, and this page shows the one for the best rung-3a code, as a look at a near-miss and not as the registered replay of a primary code. The field-off and ring-held replays were not run.
  • Validity checks. V1 the target is what is written (checked); V2 every held value is in [0, ceiling]; V3 a stored score replays to within 1e-6 mV; V4 the face and single-stripe pipelines are unchanged by the edits (checked, A8); V5 nothing existing is overwritten.

A5. The two-bump test

The user proposed a ring with two separated bumps on the top row and on the bottom row, over the ends of the flank stripes (columns 1-3 and 7-9), and nothing on the left and right sides. It was registered before it ran (7cae082, with an amendment e8a1665) and run on the same tissue, hold and score. 1,888 designed profiles were simulated.

{{BUMP_TILES}}
  • The pure two-bump profiles fail. Of 159 pure profiles, the best has an overlap of 0.557 (bump level T 1.47, everything else 1.25). None reaches 0.9.
  • The bumps write the ends of the stripes, not their length. In the best profile 34 of the 54 flank cells are dark, with 7 stray cells; the middle rows of the flanks stay light.
  • A dip between the bumps does not matter. The best uniform ring reaches 0.49, the best plateau profile 0.62 and the best pure two-bump profile 0.56, so the criterion that the dip must help by 0.10 fails.
  • A cosine fit cannot reproduce the shape at low order. Fits of the best pure profile at orders 2 to 18 reach overlaps of 0.15 to 0.33. Only at order 20, where the fit is exact for the even-only code, does the profile's own 0.56 come back.

A7. The review of the runbook before launch

The runbook RING_CODE_TARGET_PIPELINE.md was written by the session that ran the single stripe. Before reusing it, it was checked against the code and the saved training files.

Checked and correct. Every named script, data file and registration exists. The 11 registration commits match (the training registration, six amendments and a note, the mechanism, the ends and the late route). The cited line numbers are right. There are 15 stripe-named scripts. Orders 0..20 span 21 independent mirror-symmetric ring values, and the even-only set spans 11. The fallback to the face's cells when a run has no features score group is real in both analysis scripts. The wall-clock figure for the ceiling-1.3 pilot (38 minutes) matches Slurm.

Added to the runbook.

  • Two preconditions on a target: left-right mirror symmetry (the code is a cosine series, so a left-right asymmetric target cannot be reached without a sine basis), and dark features (the formation test counts cells below -34.6 mV).
  • The target-name guard in the training script covered only stripesInterior. A new target run without its own name would have written into the face's training folder.
  • Three places read if face ... else stripes: the relay network reader, the relay movie builder and the relay computation. The movie builder silently accepts any target name. The other two are guarded until the new target is added, and then become silent. Each needs an explicit branch and an else that raises.
  • The runbook's launch strings, recovered from Slurm (sacct --env-vars), replacing a reconstruction.

A correction in both directions. The first review said the single stripe's even-only 0..20 route depended on a warm library of 671,184 earlier codes. The data from the original session showed otherwise, and the numbers were recomputed here from the saved best voltages and match:

Methods

Terms

Tissue, model 1888
A simulated 11 x 11 sheet of 121 cells with ion channels and gap junctions, plus the extracellular electric field that feeds back on the channels. The trained model, number 1888, is frozen: nothing about it changes here.
Ring cells
The 40 cells on the outer edge of the sheet.
Interior cells
The 81 cells inside the ring: rows 1 to 9 and columns 1 to 9.
Vmem, mV
The membrane voltage of a cell, in millivolts. More negative is more hyperpolarised.
Dark cell
A cell with Vmem below -34.6 mV, the midpoint between the -60 mV of the target's feature cells and the -9.2 mV of its background. On the maps, shade runs from light at -5 mV to dark at -60 mV.
G_pol, G_ref
G_pol is a cell's polarising (hyperpolarising) channel conductance. Codes are written in units of G_ref, a reference conductance.
Ring code
The values the 40 ring cells' G_pol are held at, given by a cosine series in the cell's angle around the ring: a0 + a1 cos(theta) + a2 cos(2 theta) + and so on.
Order, harmonic
Order N means the series uses terms up to cos(N theta). A contiguous code of order N uses every term 0 to N.
Even-only
A code that uses only the listed even terms (0, 2, 4, and so on). A target that is symmetric left-right and top-bottom is spanned by the even terms alone.
Mirror symmetric
A cosine series gives the same ring value on the left and the right. Every code has a twin that flips the pattern top to bottom, which here gives the same double stripe.
Hold, release
For the first 301 iterations the ring cells' G_pol are overwritten with the code (the hold). Afterwards the ring follows the model's own rules (the release).
Iteration
One time step of the simulation. A run is 3,000 iterations. A replay runs longer (20,000) to see what happens after the scored window.
Ceiling
The largest G_pol a ring cell may be held at. 1.3 is the face's; 2.0 is the larger value used in arm B and every rung after it. A lone cell is forced hyperpolarised above 1.439 and has two stable voltages between 0.802 and 1.439 (its bistable window), so a ceiling above 1.439 changes what a held ring cell can do.
Flank stripes, centre stripe
The interior cut into three stripes of 3 columns by 9 rows: left flank (columns 1-3), centre (4-6), right flank (7-9). The target holds both flanks dark. On the maps the flanks are outlined with dashes.
Target, feature cells
The voltage pattern aimed at: the feature cells (here the 54 flank cells) at -60 mV, every other cell at -5 mV. The ring is scored at -5 mV even though it carries the code.
Score
Balanced root-mean-square error: the mean of the error over the feature cells and the error over all other cells, so a small pattern is not drowned out by a large background. A code's score is its lowest value after the release; the iteration where it occurs is its best moment. Lower is better. The whole interior dark scores 20.0.
Overlap, formed
Of the interior cells that are dark or in the feature cells, the share that are both. 1 means exactly the target. 0.9 or more, read at the best moment, counts as formed. The ring is not counted.
Stray cell
A dark interior cell that is not in the flank stripes.
Flood, flooded interior
A pattern with 10 or more stray cells. Here it means the whole interior dark: all 54 flank cells and all 27 centre cells, an overlap of 54/81 = 0.67 and a score of 20.0. It is counted by the overlap measure but is not two stripes.
Distinct pattern
A particular set of dark cells at a restart's best moment. Many restarts end in the same set.
Reach map
For each interior cell, the share of a chosen set of restarts in which that cell is dark at the best moment.
Neighbours
Codes made by adding a small random step (0.01 in the units the search works in) to a code's coefficients and scoring them. A code whose neighbours score much worse sits on a narrow peak.
Two-bump profile
A designed ring: one level T on the 12 ring cells at the flank ends (top and bottom rows, columns 1-3 and 7-9), M on the 6 between them, K on the 4 corners and S on the 18 side cells. M below T gives two bumps with a dip between.
Gate, G1, G2
The condition to proceed. G1: at least one restart is formed. G2: that code's score is at least 5 mV below the best of 64 random codes of the same size.
Random allowed code
A code drawn at random whose held values all lie between 0 and the ceiling. Used as a control.
CMA-ES, population, generation
The search method, which adapts a cloud of candidate codes. Each generation tries a population of candidates and moves the cloud toward the better ones.
Restart
One independent search. Even-numbered restarts start from the library, odd-numbered ones from a random allowed code.
Library
Every code already simulated in the same folder and in any listed library folders, plus stored sweeps, re-ranked by this target's score. The best ones are re-simulated and used as starting points.
Arm, rung, ladder
An arm is a set of searches with one design (arm A, B or C). The ladder is the ordered list of further stages to extend to if nothing forms, each stage being a rung (1a, 1b, 2, 3a, 3b).
Route
A way of reaching a pattern: a ceiling together with contiguous or even-only codes. The single stripe had two.
Primary code
The qualifying code with the lowest score, which the replays and mechanism analyses use. There is none for this target.
Pre-registration, amendment
Criteria and controls written in a file and committed alone before the run, so they cannot be shaped by the results. An amendment adds the details of a later stage, also committed alone before that stage runs. Anything added later is labelled exploratory.
Transfer prediction
A prediction taken from an earlier target's result, written with that result's number beside it.
Slurm array, dependency
The cluster's job system. An array runs one task per restart. A dependency starts a stage only after the previous one has ended.

Procedures

  1. Training. Each restart searches for the ring code with the lowest score. Every code is simulated for 3,000 iterations and scored from the release on. Every code evaluated is stored, so it can join the library.
  2. Reading the restarts. A table script reads each restart's stored best moment: its voltage map, ring values, score and iteration. The overlap is computed from the stored voltages with the same function the training used. Nothing is simulated or rescored. This page's galleries, stage tables, overlap chart and reach map come from that table.
  3. Summary. A summary script reads each folder and reports, per code size, the best restart, its overlap and score, the 64 random-code controls, the no-clamp tissue and the best stored sweep code, and replays the best code for 20,000 iterations. It was run for arms A and B, rungs 1 and 2 and rung 3a.
  4. Choosing the tiles. The stage tiles are the highest-overlap restart of each stage (floods left out). The gallery groups restarts by their set of dark cells, ranks the groups by their best overlap and then score, and shows the first 24.
  5. Scoring the registration. A scoring script is run once, at the end, and reports every criterion verbatim, held or failed. It has not been run yet.
  6. Mechanism. Registered separately before it runs: each cell's voltage branches and latching-switch rule, the program of who is dark when, an exact relay of which block hands the selectivity gap to which, and the relay movie. Not run for this target.
  7. Ring segments, landscape and confirmations. Registered separately: which stretch of the ring writes the pattern, where working codes sit in the space of codes, and one confirmatory test of any story the exploration suggests. Not run for this target.
