The Latching Switch

What the tissue does between releasing the boundary clamp and showing a face, reduced to one rule and four phases. Every cell is a switch with a memory; its switching window slides when its neighbours change state; and a conductance driven by the tissue's own field sweeps the whole population across that window twice. The rule is derived from the model's constants, not fitted, and it accounts for 99.1–99.6% of every cell's state at every iteration across all seven trained codes. Applied to two ensembles of 1,024 codes, it says where the face is won: in one phase, by two numbers, which the code's orders steer — separably near a program that works, not across the space as a whole.

Unless a panel says otherwise, what is drawn on this page is one run: the best trained code of orders 0–3, held for 301 iterations and released. The seven trained codes — orders 0–3 through orders 0–6 — appear only where a claim has to hold across codes, and the two ensembles only where the code space itself is the subject; every code in those is orders 0–3 as well. Each figure names its subject above the panel.

The rule

One cell, two thresholds, and a memory in between

A cell's voltage settles far faster than its polarising conductance G changes, so at every moment the cell rests at a stable root of its own voltage equation. Solving that equation with the cell's neighbours held where they are gives two critical conductances. Above G_up the depolarised root stops existing and the cell has nowhere to be but dark. Below G_down the hyperpolarised root stops existing and it has nowhere to be but light. Between them both roots exist, and the cell simply keeps whichever one it is already on. That middle band is the memory.

The thresholds are not fixed. Each neighbour pulls on the cell through its gap junctions, so a light neighbour lifts both thresholds by about 0.036 and a dark neighbour lowers them by about 0.032. A cell that goes dark therefore makes it easier for the cells beside it to go dark too. That single fact is what turns a conductance map into a sculpted pattern.

The trajectory

The conductance sweeps the window twice

With the rule fixed, everything else is the story of one number per cell. The field the tissue generates drives G up across the whole interior, then lets it collapse through the bottom of the window, which drains the first rise's darkening away; then it drives G up a second time. The first rise writes nothing — the cells it darkens are no more likely to be face cells than chance. Only on the second rise does the tissue select: the future feature cells are lifted well clear of the rest, cross G_up, and are latched as G falls back.

The sweep itself is not the clamp's doing. An unclamped run makes the same rise, fall and second rise — with a higher first peak and a trough of the same depth, reached almost 500 iterations later — so the clamp is a constraint on a tissue that polarises itself, not the thing that polarises it. It does move the sweep, and it aims the second rise; the section below separates the two.

What the clamp contributes

The tissue polarises itself; the clamp retimes it and aims it

Phase 1 reads like the clamp driving the tissue. It is not. Run the same model with no clamp at all and it makes the same excursion: the interior's mean conductance climbs from 1.00 to a peak higher than the trained code reaches, 1.481 against 1.421, falls to a trough of the same depth, and rises again. The clamp holds the sweep down and brings it forward: by the release the clamped interior sits 0.14 lower, and it reaches its trough at 585 rather than 1,055.

The clamp does not even push the boundary upward. Left alone, the ring's conductance drifts to 1.140 by the end of the hold; the trained code holds it down, to a mean of 0.817, in a particular shape. So the clamp is a constraint on a tissue that polarises itself, not the cause of the polarisation.

Most of the clamp's grip on phase 1 is not the ring's held values. As the model implements it, the clamp writes the ring's conductance and then runs one extra voltage update on every cell in the tissue, every hold iteration. A run that gets those extra updates but holds nothing on the ring reaches the release at 1.328, within 0.011 of the trained code's 1.317; a truly unclamped run is at 1.456. So of the 0.139 by which the clamp lowers the interior at release, 0.128 comes from the extra updates and 0.011 from the forty held values. The trough's timing is the reverse: the extra updates alone move it from 1,055 to 984, and the ring's shape does the rest, to 585 {MD} a uniform hold at the same mean troughs at 1,024. An earlier version of this page compared against a run that inherited the extra updates by sharing a batch with clamped runs, and reported the two as differing by 0.017; against a truly unclamped run the difference during the hold reaches 0.138.

The tissue-level loop

The tissue is an integral controller, and the field is its sensor

If the clamp does not drive the sweep, what does? Average the local law over the interior and one equation is left:

d<G>/dt
= −500 × ( <field> − 5×10−4 )

The field a cell reads is the magnitude of a vector sum, so charge adds to it: the more polarised the tissue, the larger the reading. The conductance then integrates the difference between that reading and a constant. That is a controller with a set point — and the constant it compares against is one number in the parameter file, fieldTransductionBias. Across the whole run, two coarse measures of the tissue predict the field at R² = 0.91: its mean voltage and how many cells are dark. Fine structure is invisible to it — a checkerboard at fixed mean moves the reading by less than 1%.

An integrator has no damping, which gives the trajectory its shape. The tissue overshoots its set point; the cells that darken on the way up push the reading further past it, because each dark cell raises the field; the error goes sharply negative and the conductance collapses through the bottom of the window; cells relight, the reading falls back, and the climb resumes. Phase 3 is slow precisely because the tissue is then sitting close to its set point, where the error, and so the drive, is small.

The choreography

Ordered by the field, not by contact

On the second rise the cells do not cross together. A few reach G_up on their own — they need a high conductance, because with every neighbour still light their threshold is at its highest. The rest cross later at a conductance that would not have been enough earlier, because by then some neighbour has gone dark and pulled their threshold down. Call the first kind nucleators and the second recruits: the split is a statement about the threshold each cell faced, and it is what gives the pattern its shape rather than just its extent.

It is tempting to read that as darkness spreading outward from seed cells, one block setting off the next. It is not. A neighbour moves a cell’s threshold by about 0.04; over the same rise the field moves the cell’s conductance by about 0.6. So a cell crossing while its own conductance is still climbing needs no neighbour — the drive alone reaches the bar. Only a cell crossing while its conductance is falling needs the bar brought down to meet it, and lowering the bar is the one thing a neighbour can do through contact. Through the field it does the opposite, which the measurement at the end of the causal section separates out.

The drive is not external. The conductance every cell reads is set by the extracellular field, and that field is generated by the tissue’s own voltage — every cell writes into it and reads from it at once. So ’the neighbour was not needed’ does not mean no other cell was needed. It narrows where the influence travels, not whether cells influence each other: the route is the shared field, not the gap junction to the cell next door.

So the two kinds are not a cause and its effect. Nucleator names a cell that reached the bar first, when there was no dark neighbour to be had; recruit names one that reached it later, by which time a neighbour had lowered it. The separation across the second peak is the drive’s doing as well: before the peak the conductance is rising, so the earliest crossings can only be nucleators; after it the conductance is falling, so whatever still crosses must have had help. The order is real; what carries it is the field, not contact.

Across the code space

What the orders actually steer

The same reduction applied to two ensembles of 1,024 orders 0–3 codes each: one scattered across the whole feasible region, one drawn within ±0.10 of the trained code. Every code runs the same four phases — the structure is universal. What differs is the writing in phase 3, and that is where the face is won or lost.

Two numbers describe it. Selectivity is how far the second rise lifts the face's cells above the rest. Targeting is whether the cells it lifts highest are the right ones — of the fourteen highest-conductance interior cells at the second peak, how many belong to the face. Scored at each code's own best moment rather than at a fixed iteration, these two predict the face a run reaches at R² = 0.67 near a working code.

The division of labour between the orders appears only near a working code. Scattered across the whole space, a₀ dominates almost everything and the orders do not separate — which is what the earlier knockout test found at the level of the pattern. Move to the neighbourhood of a code that works and the roles come apart cleanly.

The causal test, at the other scale

What no single cell does, the set does not do either

One cell at a time cannot see a collective cause. The drive is the tissue’s own field, so the nucleators might still produce the recruits together, at range, even though no one of them is needed by the cell beside it. The test is to block them as a set. Predictions were registered before the runner was written.

Steering

The program is steerable where it does not matter

The ensemble fit says a₁ carries phase 3 near a working code. That is an average over a thousand codes. The sharper question is whether any order is a knob — start from the trained code, move one coefficient alone, and see whether a part of the program moves with it, monotonically.

The relay

How forty ring cells reach fourteen face cells

The field cannot carry the ring’s influence to the centre directly: its screen is four cells wide, and the central 3×3 block — which holds the whole nose — is exactly out of reach. Whatever the code does to the nose, it does through other cells. This section follows that relay.

What follows is an exact decomposition of a difference: the trained run minus a baseline. The choice of baseline matters more than it looks, because the clamp does two separate things on every iteration of the hold (0–300). It writes the ring: the forty ring conductances are overwritten with the code. And it re-solves voltage for the whole tissue: after the write, embryo.py runs one extra current-and-voltage update on all 121 cells. The second is an implementation side effect, not a signal from the ring, but it is not small: A comparison against the model with no clamp at all would credit the ring with all of it, which is what the first attempt did (see What the clamp contributes, above). The baseline used here therefore has the re-solve but not the ring — the middle row below.

After iteration 300 all three follow the model’s ordinary rules, with neither effect. The real model couples the two effects, so it cannot run the middle row directly; it is built in a re-implementation of the model step (boundaryHarmonicStep.py) in which they are independent switches. That step matches the real model to within a millionth of a millivolt over all 2,174 iterations with both effects off (no clamp) and with both on (trained), so the middle row is a combination of two checked parts, not a separate model.

One step of the model gives the difference exactly as D(n+1) = J̄(n)·D(n) + src(n), where J̄ is the step’s Jacobian averaged over the straight segment between the two states and src is what writing the ring changes at that step, with the re-solve already present in both runs. Nothing is linearised, so the decomposition holds for the real, finite difference. Carrying a readout back through it says, at every moment, how much of the final selectivity gap each cell’s deviation is holding — and after the release those shares sum to the same total at every iteration, which is the check that the bookkeeping is exact.

The tissue is 121 cells with a dense field coupling (every cell reaches every grid point each step) plus local gap junctions — 14,520 possible directed pairs of influence. What follows is not a survey of that space; it is the small part of it the ring’s isolated effect actually uses, found by carrying the exact difference back through the real trajectory rather than assumed in advance. Coarse-graining the relay

The relay does not survive averaging — but the wall has two halves

The relay above was inferred cell by cell, on all 121 cells. Would a coarser picture do? Average the lattice in b×b squares — 2×2, 3×3, on up to the whole 11×11 lattice averaged into a 2×2 grid — and see whether a simpler pathway survives. Eleven is not a multiple of any of these, so each size has one shorter band of blocks that can sit anywhere; every placement was run (36 tilings of 2×2 squares, 16 of 3×3, 9 each of 4×4 and 5×5, 4 of the 2×2 grid).

Two different things can be meant by a coarse relay, and only one of them can be wrong. Re-cutting the books sums, for each block, the shares its cells hold and the transfers between blocks. It is exact, adds to the same 0.356 at every resolution, and hides only what happens inside a block: a description. A closed block model is a model. Give every block one voltage and one conductance, average the relay’s own step over blocks, run it forward from nothing with the clamp’s write averaged the same way, and ask whether it still produces the gap and its rise. Nothing is fitted; the one approximation is that a block’s cells share a value. Only this one would count as a simpler pathway. It was registered before it was run (data/boundaryHarmonicCoarseGrainPredictions…json): a final gap within 10%, a running total within 15%, and the same routes between blocks as the exact books.

What survives: the wall

How well it holds

Where the rule is right, and where it is only nearly right

The test is one step ahead: given the tissue's true state, does the rule say what each cell does next? Across all seven trained codes it is right for 99.1% to 99.6% of cell-moments. The residual is not a missing mechanism — it is timing jitter at the crossing itself.

shows · all seven trained codes, orders 0–3 through orders 0–6

What this does not do. Run open-loop from the release, the reduced model drifts: it recovers the final pattern to about 79 of 81 interior cells, but not every event, because a threshold missed by a hair changes a neighbour count and the error compounds in a system already known to be extremely sensitive. The rule is a statement about each transition given the true state, not a claim that two thousand iterations can be replayed from bits alone.

The conductance itself is taken as given here. It is driven by the extracellular field, which the tissue's own voltage generates. The clamp control settles what that loop does not owe to the code — the whole sweep, since an unclamped run makes it — and pins the code's contribution to the second rise's aim. What it does not explain is how a profile held on 40 boundary cells, hundreds of iterations earlier, ends up selecting those particular 14 interior cells. That step is the remaining question, not a solved one.

Methods

Terms, and how each one is measured

Everything below is measured on replays of the same trained model, 1888, on an 11 × 11 lattice with a 301-iteration hold. Scripts are named at the end of each group.

The tissue

Lattice, ring, interior
121 cells in an 11 × 11 square lattice, each gap-junction coupled to its four nearest neighbours, with no wrap-around at the edges. The 40 cells on the outer edge are the ring; the other 81 are the interior. All counts on this page are over the interior unless stated.
Feature cells, background
The 14 interior cells the face target wants hyperpolarised — two eyes, a nose, a mouth. The other 67 interior cells are the background.
Vmem
Membrane voltage in millivolts. Lower is more hyperpolarised, and drawn darker.
G, G_pol / G_ref
A cell's polarising channel conductance, always quoted as a ratio to G_ref = 1 nS. It is a state variable, not a constant: the extracellular field drives it on every iteration. Written G throughout.
Hold, release, ring code
For iterations 0–300 each ring cell's G is overwritten every iteration with its value in the code. From iteration 301 the ring follows the model's own rules. The code is a₀ + a₁ cos θ + … + aₙ cos Nθ over a ring cell's angle θ from straight up.

The switch rule

Quasi-static reduction
Voltage settles far faster than G or a cell's neighbours change, so at each moment a cell is taken to sit at a stable root of its own dV/dt — the full ion-channel and gap-junction equation, with the neighbours held at their current values. Roots are found by evaluating the current on a 1,201-point voltage grid from −70 to +5 mV and taking every downward zero crossing.
Branch, dark, light
The stable root a cell is nearest. A cell is dark when that root lies below −30 mV. That cut is not chosen: pooled over all cells and moments, the roots are bimodal with an empty band from about −45 to −18 mV, and −30 sits inside it. The branch label agrees with the −34.6 mV scoring threshold on 95–96% of cell-moments; they differ only while a cell is in flight between branches, which is why they part company most at the trough, where almost everything is in flight. Every cell count on this page is the branch label, including both lines of the count panel under the trajectory figure, so the two are comparable; face quality is the exception and uses the −34.6 mV cut, because that is what the score itself uses.
G_up, G_down
The two conductances at which a cell's fixed-point structure changes. Above G_up only the dark root exists; below G_down only the light one; between them both exist. Found by scanning G over 4,001 values from 0 to 2 and recording where the stable-root count changes between 2 and 1, with neighbours set to archetype voltages of −50 mV (dark) and −10 mV (light). Substituting those archetypes for the neighbours' true voltages preserves the root count on 97.5% of cell-moments and the branch on 98.7%.
Neighbour shift
Both thresholds move with the neighbourhood, fitted over all 15 (degree, dark-count) cases as G_down = 0.803 + 0.036·light − 0.032·dark and G_up = 1.439 + 0.035·light − 0.040·dark. At zero neighbours these give 0.803 and 1.439, reproducing the isolated-cell window the model's own code records independently as 0.802 and 1.439.
Latching window
The interval between the two thresholds, where both states exist and a cell keeps the one it is on. A cell is latched when it has been driven out of the window and has returned into it holding its new state.
One-step accuracy
The share of cell-moments at which the rule, applied to the tissue's true state, names the cell's true state. Evaluated at every interior cell and every iteration from 0 to the code's best moment. Free-running the rule instead is a different and much weaker test, and is reported separately.

The program

Phases
Located from the interior's mean G. The trough is its minimum over iterations 302–1,300; the second peak is its maximum from the trough onwards; the first peak is its maximum over the hold. Phase boundaries are these, not fixed iterations. "First peak" names when it happens, not what causes it — an unclamped run reaches a higher one.
Selectivity
How far the second rise separates the face from the rest. The mean G over the 14 feature cells minus the mean over the 67 background cells, evaluated at that run's own second peak. Units are G_pol / G_ref. Zero means the rise lifts feature and background alike; negative means it favours the background.
Targeting
Whether the cells it lifts highest are the right ones. Rank the 81 interior cells by G at the second peak and count how many of the top 14 are feature cells. Runs 0 to 14; chance is 14 × 14 / 81 ≈ 2.4. Selectivity is a difference of group means and targeting is an overlap of sets, so a code can score well on one and badly on the other.
Nucleator, recruit
Of the cells that go dark after iteration 1,200, a nucleator had no dark neighbour at the moment it crossed and so faced its highest threshold; a recruit had at least one, and crossed at a bar its neighbours had already lowered. The split describes the threshold each cell faced; it is not a claim that darkness spread from one to the other, which two interventions test separately: one cell at a time, and the whole set of nucleators at once. The cut at 1,200 is a fixed iteration rather than a landmark of the run: it sits between the churn that follows the trough — where darkenings are reversed almost as fast as they happen — and the settled rise that produces the scored pattern. In six of the seven codes every cell dark at the scored moment crosses after it; the orders 0–1 run has one cell that does not, of the 20 it ends dark.
Necessary, in the causal sense
Exploratory — written after the result it examines, with no predictions registered beforehand. A neighbour can only move a cell's threshold, by about 0.04, while the field moves the cell's conductance by about 0.6 over the same rise. So a crossing on a rising conductance needs no neighbour, and only a crossing on a falling one does. The slope is the change in that cell's own G over the 10 iterations either side of the crossing. The link test pairs each blocked cell with every neighbour that crossed later with at least one dark neighbour, and asks whether blocking changed that neighbour's state at the scored moment. Script: analyzeBoundaryHarmonicRecruitmentNecessity11x11.py.
The two channels
Exploratory. One cell is moved from light to −40 mV in a real recorded tissue state and the extracellular field is recomputed exactly, at five moments and over every fourth interior cell as probe. Because eV is the magnitude of a vector sum it is always positive, so charge adds to it; with a transduction gain of −1, and a relaxation term carrying units of siemens against a target of order one, what the field sets is a rate: d(G/G_ref)/dt ≈ −500 × (field − 0.0005). A dark cell therefore drives its neighbours' conductance down and depolarises them, while through the gap junctions it lowers their G_up and helps them cross. This is measured as a derivative rather than from the interventions because blocking eleven cells at once in a chaotic system gives a difference that is divergence, not coupling. Script: analyzeBoundaryHarmonicFieldCoupling11x11.py.
Set point
fieldTransductionBias, the constant the field reading is compared against in the transduction. The interior's mean conductance integrates the difference, so <G> turns over exactly where the interior mean field crosses it. The sweep holds everything else fixed — same trained code, one batch — and varies only this constant, at 0.6, 0.8, 0.9, 1.0, 1.1, 1.2 and 1.4 times its trained value. Predictions were registered in boundaryHarmonicSetPointPredictions…json before the runner was written. P2 and P4 failed outright; P3 is recorded as failed on its registered wording, which searched for the first peak inside the hold, and holds at every run's true turning point. Scripts: runBoundaryHarmonicSetPointSweep11x11.py, analyzeBoundaryHarmonicSetPointSweep11x11.py.
The relay
An exact decomposition of the trained run minus a baseline, carried through boundaryHarmonicStep.py (a pure re-implementation of one model iteration, checked against the model to 1.6×10−15 per step, with a hand-derived Jacobian checked against autograd to 6×10−16). The baseline is extraUpdateOnly: the tissue-wide voltage re-solve the clamp performs every hold iteration, present, but the ring never held — isolating the ring's own held values from that re-solve, which a first attempt against a fully unclamped baseline could not tell apart (see What the clamp contributes). Registered in boundaryHarmonicRingOnlyRelayPredictions…json before any state from this baseline was computed; the updated causal predictions were set from the first attempt's own published, registered results, and the two spatial predictions (B1, B2) were reasoned from the lattice's shell structure alone. R1 fails its literal threshold, honestly — the remainder is the same-step voltage response to holding the ring, not a leftover confound. B1 and B2 fail: the imprint never reaches the nose's own shell depth on a tissue-wide average, which is consistent with, not contrary to, R4 holding (a thin but causally decisive thread reaches the nose on schedule while most of the tissue stays shallow). Quadrature splits at every G_pol clip crossing and at every near-zero passage of a grid point's field vector (found while chasing 2×10−5 residuals down to 8.8×10−7). The network figures draw net transfers (what one cell hands another minus what it receives back), ranked separately for the field and for gap junctions, because the strongest gap-junction transfer is about a fifth the size of the strongest field one and a shared ranking never reaches them. The movie is an initial state (iteration 0, both runs identical, so every held share is 0), thirty-six frames, one per saved 50-iteration window from iteration 0 to the readout, and a final state. In each window the arrows are the transfers summed over it and the colours are read at its end; during the hold (the first seven windows) the clamp also injects share into the ring, recovered exactly as a cell’s change minus its transfers, and those injections total the final gap. The default view is a continuous-time interpolation of those frames (colours and chart linear between window ends, transfer rates linear between window centres, zero at the start and at the readout) with particles streaming along the transfers on a canvas layer; it is exact at window ends and centres and an approximation between them. A truly finer time step would need the decomposition re-run with narrower edge windows. Scripts: computeBoundaryHarmonicRelay11x11.py --baseline extraUpdateOnly, analyzeBoundaryHarmonicRingOnlyRelay11x11.py.
Coarse-graining the relay
Block, tiling. A block is a set of cells treated as one; a tiling cuts the lattice into b×b squares from a corner. Eleven is not a multiple of b, so one band of each size is shorter and every position for it is run (74 tilings for b = 2…6). Re-cut books (the aggregated relay): the relay’s own shares and transfers summed over blocks; exact, always adds to the same gap. Closed block model (the reduced relay): with P averaging Vmem and G_pol over each block and Q copying a block’s value to its cells, the block difference is evolved by y(n+1) = (P·J̄(n)·Q)·y(n) + P·src(n) from zero, read out with the readout copied the same way, and its own adjoint, shares and transfers are computed from P·J̄·Q. It coarse-grains the exact difference dynamics along the fine pair of runs; it is not a re-simulation of a nonlinear block model. Final-gap error: the model’s final selectivity gap against the fine 0.35589, relative. Running-total error: the root-mean-square difference, over all 1,767 states, between the model’s readout curve and the fine curve, relative to the final gap. Route (pathway cosine): the cosine between the model’s net transfers between block pairs and the exact books’, in each of the clear and write phases. Bar: a final-gap error within 10% (chosen by the user), a running-total error within 15% and both routes with cosine at least 0.8. State floor: the final-gap error from block-averaging the exact final state and reading it out, before any dynamics. Source floor: the error in the gap the exact adjoint assigns to the clamp’s write once the write is block-averaged. Net transfer retained: the share of the exact relay’s summed net transfer between cell pairs that still shows as net transfer between blocks. Scattered control: 200 partitions with the same block sizes as a tiling but cells assigned at random (seed 20250925). Cells grouped by what they do: contiguous Ward clustering of the cells on their difference and share over time (exploratory). Greedy search: from the lattice, repeatedly merge the two neighbouring blocks whose merger costs the model least, judged on the selectivity readout alone or on selectivity, nose, eyes and mouth together (exploratory); a partition is scored by its own model at every size. Proper orthogonal decomposition: a closed model on the leading patterns of the difference, voltage and conductance first scaled to comparable size. Pruned links: the relay’s recursion with all cell-to-cell couplings removed except the strongest ones, by summed size of the relay’s transfers. Exact share of a wall cell: run forward, alone, only the write one ring cell received; the forty runs add to the whole difference, so each cell owns a share of the gap. It is accounting on the actual pair of runs. Held alone and left out: runs of the model itself in which only a given part of the ring, or all but that part, is held to the trained code’s values through iterations 0–300, against the same extraUpdateOnly baseline as the relay. Additivity of a half split: the two halves of twenty contiguous ring cells held alone, summed, minus the whole ring, over the whole ring. Registered in boundaryHarmonicCoarseGrainPredictions…json (squares, the scattered control, the wall and the decision rule) and boundaryHarmonicWallCounterfactualPredictions…json before their runs. Exploratory, unregistered: the grouped cells, both searches, the modes, the pruned links, the exact wall shares and their segmentation, and the scan of every half split. The V1 registration was too tight (see the note under the table). Scripts: computeBoundaryHarmonicRelay11x11.py --jacobianPath (keeps every step’s Jacobian; about fifteen minutes and 1.5 GB, not committed), boundaryHarmonicCoarseGrain.py, analyzeBoundaryHarmonicCoarseGrain11x11.py, exploreBoundaryHarmonicCoarseGrain11x11.py, searchBoundaryHarmonicCoarseGrain11x11.py, analyzeBoundaryHarmonicWallWiring11x11.py, analyzeBoundaryHarmonicWallCounterfactual11x11.py; the library’s algebra is checked on a synthetic system by checkBoundaryHarmonicCoarseGrain.py.

Aggregate block
Every nucleator of the second rise held at 1.25 over iterations 1,250–2,173 in one run, scored on how many of the ten recruits the untouched run leaves dark are still dark at 2,173. The ladder blocks the first k nucleators by crossing time. Face quality
Structural intersection-over-union between the dark interior cells and the 14 feature cells — which cells are dark, never how dark. Scored at each run's own best moment, the maximum over iterations sampled every 5 from release to the end. Codes reach their second peak anywhere from iteration 900 to 2,900, so scoring them all at the trained code's moment hides most of the signal: the scattered ensemble's best rises from 0.545 to 0.867 under the fair readout.

1,024 codes with a₀ uniform on [0.3, 1.3] and a₁–a₃ uniform on [−0.6, 0.6], rejected unless every held value lands in [0.02, 1.98]; seed 17.
Slice ensemble
1,024 codes, each the trained orders 0–3 code plus a uniform draw on [−0.10, 0.10] in every coefficient, same rejection rule; seed 23, with the trained code itself first. A distance control is reported with it, since a slice centred on a working code could make "better" mean only "closer".
Clamp conditions
Free applies no clamp at any iteration. Uniform, mean holds all 40 ring cells at the trained code's mean. Uniform, ceiling holds them all at 1.3, the best order-0 code. Trained is the orders 0–3 code. All four are replayed in one batch under identical conditions, so only the clamp differs.
Interventions
One cell's G is overwritten every iteration from 1,250 to 2,173, in an otherwise untouched run. Block sets 1.25, inside the window for every neighbourhood. Force sets 1.65, above G_up for every neighbourhood. No-op sets the value the cell had in the untouched run, and is the control on the machinery itself.

Statistics

Cross-validated R²
Five-fold, on a design of the four standardised coefficients plus all ten squares and interactions, with an intercept. Folds are assigned by a fixed permutation, so every quantity on this page is split the same way. Negative values mean the fit predicts held-out codes worse than their mean does.
Drop-one loss
The full R² minus the R² refitted without one coefficient and all its quadratic terms. It is what that order carries that the others cannot supply, so the losses need not sum to the full R².
Shuffled-code null
The same fit with the codes permuted against the outcomes, which gives the R² a design of this size earns by chance. It sits near −0.02 throughout.
Monotone control
A single order counts as a knob on a quantity when, across the sweep of that order alone from −0.30 to +0.30, Spearman's ρ exceeds 0.8 in absolute value at p < 0.05. The sweep moves one coefficient and holds the other three at their trained values.
Determinism
Every replay is deterministic given its code, so these R² measure how smoothly a quantity depends on the four coefficients under a quadratic model, not how much information survives. A flexible enough model would fit everything. Comparisons on this page use one estimator throughout, so they remain like-for-like.
