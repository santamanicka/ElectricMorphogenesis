The Tilted Dial

The boundary dial, one G_pol value shared by all 40 ring cells, is order 0 of the ring code. This page adds order 1 on top: a gradient that raises one side of the ring and lowers the other. Every held value stays between 0 and 1.3, the stretch where the 301-iteration hold changed most smoothly. First question: what does the gradient do to the landscape of patterns?

Step 1 · The landscape

The patterns

The tissue is an 11 × 11 lattice of cells. Its ring cells are the 40 cells on the outer edge, and during the hold (the first 301 iterations) each ring cell's polarising conductance is fixed at a chosen value. The dial (DC) gives every ring cell the same value. The gradient (G) adds more on one side of the ring and takes the same amount off the opposite side. Every held value stays between 0 and 1.3. Each thumbnail below shows what the interior settles into long after the hold, for one combination of dial and gradient. Dark cells are hyperpolarised. All terms are defined under Methods.

The code

Precisely, each ring cell is held at DC + G·cos(θ − φ), where θ is the cell's angle about the lattice centre and φ is the direction the gradient points, both measured clockwise from straight up. The cell facing φ holds DC + G and the cell opposite holds DC − G. Keeping every held value between 0 and 1.3 means G ≤ DC and G ≤ 1.3 − DC, so the allowed codes fill a triangle. Because the model has the square's symmetries, directions from 0° to 45° cover every case: 0° points at the middle of an edge, 45° at a corner, and 22.5° lies between.

Finding 1

The gradient acts only where it pushes ring cells past the dial's own jumps

On its own, the dial moves the pattern below 1.3 mainly in six jumps, at 0.695, 0.775, 1.195 and 1.245–1.265. A gradient spreads the ring's values over DC − G to DC + G, so each ring cell sits at its own point on the dial. The pattern then changes where, and only where, some ring cells' held values cross one of those jumps. The strip below follows one line through the triangle, first with the dial alone and then with a gradient.

The same holds across the whole triangle. A step of 0.02 in DC or G that carries no ring cell across a jump moves the pattern by a median of only 0.12–0.14 mV. Steps that carry cells across account for 76–88% of all movement (Appendix A2). Grouping codes by which side of each jump every ring cell sits on explains 90–94% of pattern variance. So the landscape is a patchwork: nearly flat patches, joined at seams laid down by the dial's own jumps. Before a seam the gradient's small effect turns with its direction; past one the pattern tips, and about half of the change is dial-like (Appendix A3).

Finding 2

Past those seams it reaches patterns the dial alone never makes

From 0 to 1.3 the dial alone keeps one interior motif. Binarised at the single-cell saddle (−29.3 mV), every pattern has the same five dark cells: one two cells in from each corner and one at the centre (Appendix A1). The dial shades the grey levels around them but never redraws them. The gradient does redraw them.

Step 1 · The storyline

The story so far, in plain terms

The setup. Think of the 40 edge cells as a ring of dimmer switches around a room, and the interior pattern as the lighting that results. The dial turns all 40 switches together. The gradient tilts them, turning one side of the ring up and the opposite side down, like tilting a tray.

The dial alone clicks. Turned together, the switches mostly just shade the room: from 0 to 1.3 the interior keeps the same five dark dots and only the grey around them drifts. At a few settings the pattern clicks into a new arrangement, like a dial with detents. The clicks sit at 0.695, 0.775, 1.195 and 1.245–1.265.

Tilting only matters at those clicks. A small tilt nudges the pattern by a fraction of a millivolt. Once the high side of the ring passes a click, the pattern jumps. Mapped over every combination of dial and tilt, the landscape looks like terraced ground: flat terraces separated by steps, and every step sits where some edge cells cross one of the dial's clicks. The tilt adds no clicks of its own. It lets the edge cells pass the dial's clicks a few at a time instead of all at once.

That is where new patterns come from. Moving every switch together gives essentially one pattern family. Pushing only part of the ring past a click gives combinations the dial can never make. It is like a piano: the dial presses every key at once, only louder or softer, while the tilt presses a run of neighbouring keys, and many more chords become available. The grid found 163 distinct interior motifs instead of one. Some patterns lie farther from anything the dial makes than the dial's own patterns lie from each other.

The catch, and what comes next. The new chords come in steps, not as a smooth slide. Along any path, the tilt breaks one big click into several smaller ones, and the pattern travels nearly three times as far overall. Step 2 asks whether that makes a finer steering handle or only a bumpier one. A face is left–right symmetric, so only an up–down tilt fits it, which leaves just two knobs: dial and tilt. Step 3 asks whether two knobs can reach a face, or whether more ways of shaping the edge (higher harmonics) are needed.

Appendix · supporting analyses

A1 · The dial alone, below 1.3

With the 301-iteration hold, turning the dial from 0 to 1.3 moves the pattern mostly in jumps. There are six: at 0.695 (6.3 mV RMS between dials 0.01 apart), 0.775 (2.0 mV), 1.195 (2.1 mV), and a cluster at 1.245–1.265 (up to 6.5 mV). Between jumps it moves about 0.15 mV per 0.02 of dial. The two patterns farthest apart, at 0.69 and 1.30, differ by 8.8 mV RMS. All of them share the five-dot motif.

A2 · The seams, cell by cell

Every ring cell has its own seams, where its held value reaches a jump. One grid step (0.02 in DC or in G) carries some ring cells across a jump, or none.

A3 · Before a seam the pattern turns with the gradient; past one it tips

Any change can be split into three parts by how it behaves under the square's symmetries. The part that flips sign under a half turn (circular orders 1, 3, 5, …) is what an order-1 clamp drives directly, and it turns with the gradient. The part unchanged by a quarter turn (orders 0, 4, 8, …) is what a uniform dial moves. The part left over (orders 2, 6, 10, …) is neither.

A4 · The gain in variety does not hinge on the threshold

Binarising a time-averaged pattern is sensitive to cells that sit near the threshold, so motifs were counted at four thresholds. The spread of the patterns was also measured without any threshold, as the number of independent directions they use.

Methods

Terms

Lattice, cell
The tissue is an 11 × 11 square lattice of 121 cells, indexed row by row from the top-left (row 0 at the top, column 0 at the left). Each cell is coupled by gap junctions to its four nearest neighbours; the lattice does not wrap around at its edges.
Ring cells
The 40 cells on the lattice's outer edge: all of row 0, row 10, column 0 and column 10. These are the only cells the clamp acts on.
Interior cells
The other 81 cells, rows and columns 1 to 9. Motifs are read from these.
Vmem
A cell's membrane voltage, in millivolts (mV). Lower (more negative) is more hyperpolarised and is drawn darker.
G_pol, G_ref
G_pol is the conductance of a cell's polarising ion channel, the channel that pulls Vmem down. G_ref = 1 nS is the reference conductance. All held values on this page are G_pol / G_ref. The model keeps G_pol between 0 and 2 G_ref, so held values must lie in [0, 2]; this page keeps them in [0, 1.3].
Hold, release
During the hold, iterations 0 to 300 (301 iterations), each ring cell's G_pol is overwritten on every iteration with its held value × G_ref. From iteration 301 on (release), the ring cells follow the model's own dynamics like every other cell.
Code
The 40 held values. On this page every code has the form DC + G·cos(θ − φ).
Dial (DC)
The order-0 part of the code: one value shared by all 40 ring cells. "DC" is borrowed from electronics, where it means "direct current", the constant part of a signal. Here it is the ring's average held value, the level around which the gradient term G·cos(θ − φ) varies.
Gradient (G), direction (φ)
The order-1 part: G·cos(θ − φ), a single cosine around the ring with amplitude G, highest on the side facing φ. θ is a ring cell's angle about the lattice centre and φ the gradient's direction, both measured clockwise from straight up (toward row 0). "The side the gradient raises" is the side facing φ.
Circular order
A pattern of values around a closed loop decomposed into cosines and sines: order 0 is the mean, order 1 one cycle around the loop, order k k cycles.
Ring range
A code's lowest to highest held value, DC − G to DC + G at 0° and 45° (slightly less at 22.5°, where no ring cell faces φ exactly).
Late pattern
Each cell's mean Vmem over iterations 2000 to 2999 of a 3000-iteration run: the readout for every result here, and what every thumbnail shows.
No clamp
The same model and initial state run with no hold at all, read over the same iterations.
Dial alone, dial sweep
Codes with G = 0, run at every dial value from 0 to 2 in steps of 0.01 with the same hold and readout. Only the part from 0 to 1.3 is used here.
Twin
A code's twin is the dial-alone run at the same DC.
RMS
Root mean square. The RMS difference between two patterns is the square root of the mean, over the 121 cells, of the squared difference in Vmem, in mV.
Change, Δ
The RMS difference between a code's late pattern and its twin's.
Step
The RMS difference between the late patterns of two codes one grid step apart: 0.02 apart in DC at the same G, or 0.02 apart in G at the same DC. "Movement" is the sum of step sizes.
Jump
A place where the dial alone changes the pattern abruptly: a step of more than 2 mV RMS between dial values 0.01 apart, located at the midpoint. Below 1.3 there are six, at 0.695, 0.775, 1.195, 1.245, 1.255 and 1.265.
Quiet region, wedge, ceiling band
Three regions of the (DC, G) triangle, set by a code's ring range. Quiet: the range contains no jump. Wedge: it contains the jump at 0.695 or 0.775 but its top stays below 1.195. Each of those jumps J opens a V-shaped region G ≥ |DC − J| upward from DC = J, and the two Vs overlap into one wedge. Ceiling band: the range's top reaches the cluster at 1.195–1.265, a strip along the triangle's right-hand edges.
Spans a jump
A code spans a jump when a jump lies within its ring range, so that some ring cells are held below it and others above.
Crosses a jump, seam
Between two codes, a ring cell crosses a jump when its held value lies on opposite sides of that jump in the two codes. A seam is the set of codes at which one ring cell's held value equals a jump value; in the (DC, G) triangle it is a straight line. The dashed lines in the maps are the seams of the highest and lowest ring cells.
Symmetry classes, odd share
The square has eight symmetries: rotations by 0°, 90°, 180° and 270°, and four mirrors. A change is split into its part that flips sign under a half turn (180°), and a remainder. The remainder is split into its part that flips sign under a quarter turn (90°) and its part left unchanged by one. On each concentric ring of cells these three parts hold circular orders 1, 3, 5, …; 2, 6, 10, …; and 0, 4, 8, …. The first is what an order-1 clamp drives directly, and the last is what a uniform dial moves ("dial-like"). The odd share is the first part's share of the change's summed squares.
Off the track
The RMS difference between a code's late pattern and the nearest of the 131 dial-alone late patterns from 0 to 1.3 (steps of 0.01).
Principal components
The directions of greatest spread in the set of all patterns, each pattern treated as a list of 121 numbers: 131 dial-alone patterns and 3,168 gradient codes, centred on their mean.
Effective dimensions
How many principal components a set of patterns meaningfully spreads over, measured as the participation ratio (Σλ)² / Σλ², where λ are the variances along the principal components.
Saddle, bistable window
A single isolated cell with G_pol / G_ref below 0.802 has one resting state (about −7 mV). Between 0.802 and 1.439 it has two, near −50 and −10 mV, separated by an unstable point, the saddle, at about −29.3 mV. Above 1.439 it has one hyperpolarised state.
Motif
The set of interior cells whose late Vmem is below a threshold, −29.3 mV (the saddle) unless stated. Two motifs are "copies" when one of the square's eight symmetries carries one onto the other; "copies merged" counts each such family once.
Churn
A cell's Vmem standard deviation over iterations 2000 to 2999: how much it still moves within the readout window.
Storyline words
The plain-language section uses "edge cells" for ring cells, "tilt" for the gradient, "clicks" for jumps and "terraces" for the flat patches between seams.

Procedures

Model
Checkpoint 1888 from the band-hold face-training runs: G_pol-only clamp on the ring, trained with a 100-iteration hold and the facialFeatureBalanced objective. Its learned code is replaced by the codes above. Its tissue parameters are kept unchanged: gap-junction strength 0.05 and the bioelectric field enabled with screen size 4 and transduction weight 1000. So is its initial state, Vmem −9.2 mV on every cell.
Simulation
Time step 0.01, 3000 iterations. Runs replay the model with the same call sequence as the Boundary Dial report, which reproduces the checkpoint's stored training loss. During the hold each iteration runs the model's normal update, then writes the held values and updates currents and Vmem once more.
Grid
DC from 0 to 1.30 and G from 0.02 to min(DC, 1.3 − DC), both multiples of 0.02: 1,056 codes for each of φ = 0°, 22.5° and 45°, 3,168 in all. Held values that rounding put a hair below 0 were set to 0. Runs were split across 64 cluster jobs, and the results were merged and checked for completeness.
Symmetry checks
Four codes (DC 0.30 with G 0.20, 0.64 with 0.40, 1.00 with 0.24, and 1.20 with 0.10) were also run at φ = 90°, 180°, −22.5° and 67.5°. Each was compared with the grid run it should equal after a quarter turn, a half turn or a mirror. The largest difference was 0.00024 mV. Codes at 0° and 45° keep a mirror symmetry, which holds to within 0.35 mV at worst.
Seam statistics
Every pair of grid neighbours (0.02 apart in DC or in G, including the dial-alone row) gives one step, labelled with how many ring cells cross a jump between the two codes. The grouping test puts codes with every ring cell on the same side of every jump into one group. It reports the share of pattern variance between groups, compared with the same number of k-means clusters of the codes' (DC, G) positions (five seeds).
Motif counts
Distinct motifs over the dial-alone patterns from 0 to 1.3 and over all gradient codes, at −26.3, −29.3, −32.3 and −34.6 mV. The gallery merges copies and shows, for each motif, the code whose pattern is closest to the mean of all codes with that motif.
Caveat: churn
The tissue is still moving in the readout window. Interior churn averages about 5.8 mV for the median code and 6.0 mV on the dial alone, and the unclamped tissue drifts as well. Every pattern and motif here is a time average over that window.
