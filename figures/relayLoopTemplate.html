Companion to the relay movie · custom layout

The relay's loop, symmetric and in order

The movie's custom layout draws only each block's single strongest transfer, so the picture on screen names one side of every pair — “top‑right,” never “top‑left and top‑right.” The full, untruncated numbers underneath are exactly mirrored. This redraws the causal chain with both twins restored, against this report's own phases — flood, clear, write and latch — and lays the three the relay actually covers into one network instead of one frame at a time.

Why the display looks one-sided

Every named block keeps at most one outgoing arrow per channel — the rule this layout was built on, so a block never looks like it fires twice. Checked against the full, untruncated field-transfer matrix (not the pruned movie data), ring top → background top‑left and ring top → background top‑right come out equal to about eight significant figures in every one of the 36 windows — not just on average. The two differ only by floating-point noise around 10−8, roughly a billion times smaller than the transfer itself. Whichever one the display happens to draw is that noise breaking an exact tie, not a real asymmetry. The same holds for every mirror pair checked: the two background quadrants, the two ring sides, and every self-paired block's (ring top, ring bottom, eyes, nose, mouth) transfers to each side of a pair.

By default the particles run the full sequence on a loop — flood, a pause while it clears, clear, a pause, write, then the longest pause before flood begins again — each phase's transfers lighting up, streaming, and fading fully before the next one starts. Click a phase to isolate it: its own particles then flow continuously and the rest hold still. Click again to return to the sequence. Latch has no chip: it runs iteration 1765–2173, after this relay's own last saved window, so there is nothing of its own to draw here — see the note below the loop.

The gray cells behind the network are the tissue itself: each cell shaded by its membrane voltage at the end of whichever phase is live (iteration 301 for flood, 585 for clear, 1765 for write), for the ring code on show — white for the most depolarized, black for the most hyperpolarized, the “dark” cells of the report. The outlined cells are the face. The eyes node sits midway between the two eyes, the nose and mouth nodes at the centres of theirs, so each node marks its own feature on the picture underneath.

The ribbon above the network is the conductance the relay explains: the mean G_pol / G_ref of the 14 feature cells (orange) and of the other 67 interior cells (gray), as in figure 2 of the latching-switch report, for the ring code on show. The shaded band is the spread across the cells of each group (lowest to highest); the shading between the two means is the selectivity gap. Dashed lines are the trained code, for comparison. The marker sweeps each phase in turn, so it moves faster through write than through flood — the phases last very different numbers of iterations. “Difference from trained” plots each group’s mean minus the trained code’s, so a flat line on zero means no change. In slider mode the thin lines are the other six stops, labelled by their coefficient multiple.

Reading the loop, phase by phase

Flood (it. 0–301, the clamp writing the ring): the ring's top side pushes straight out into both top corners — the one clearly one-way transfer of the whole run. The mouth already nudges the ring's bottom a little even here, and the corners leak a little into the eyes.

Clear (301–585): the direct push shuts off, and what it seeded takes over — the top corners now drain hard into the eyes and into the nose, and feed back into the ring's sides. The trough (≈585) sits at the end of this phase: a second channel opens low in the tissue — the ring's bottom and sides begin pushing into the bottom corners — just as the top corners' pull on the eyes and nose stalls.

Write (585–1765, most of the run, and the relay's last saved window): the bottom channel from the trough is now the main one and still growing. A new edge closes the loop, bottom corners into top. The eyes and the nose both reverse outright — discharging into the top corners instead of drawing from them, the exact opposite of the clear phase, both features flipping the same way. The mouth's one real inflow, from the bottom corners, also arrives only here.

Latch (1765–2173): outside this relay. The relay's own movie stops at the readout, iteration 1765, so there is no transfer data for the phase in which the pattern is reported to set. Nothing said here extends to it.

It is not a single baton pass from ring to face. It is a short loop — ring sides, both background corner pairs, the eyes and the nose — that reverses direction once, at the trough, and keeps circulating through the write phase. The mouth is a late, minor rider on that circulation, not a link in a chain that runs through it.
