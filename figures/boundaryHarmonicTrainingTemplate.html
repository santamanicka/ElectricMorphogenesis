Training the Ring

The Tilted Dial described ring codes by their harmonics: the dial (order 0), a tilt (order 1), an oval (order 2). This page trains such codes directly toward a face, from the dial alone up to orders 0–6, with an evolutionary search. A rough face needs order 1. A clean face, every feature cell dark and nothing else, needs order 3 and is complete by order 4 or 5. But it is a moment the tissue passes through, not a pattern it keeps.

The patterns

The best face at each order

Each code holds the 40 ring cells (the outer edge) at a mirror-symmetric profile, a₀ + a₁ cos θ + … + a_N cos Nθ, for the first 301 iterations, then releases them. Training searched the coefficients for the lowest balanced RMS against the target at any single iteration after release. Shown: the target; the tissue with no clamp; the best code of the earlier sweeps; and the best trained code of each size, at its best moment. All terms are defined under Methods.

Finding 1

A clean face needs order 3; orders 1 and 2 give a rough one, and more than 4 add nothing

The dial alone reaches 21.8 mV, by darkening the whole tissue just after release: half the error goes, but it is not a face. Adding a tilt (order 1) brings 17.1 mV and the first face: two eyes, a nose and a blob of a mouth (structural IoU 0.55, part score 5.3 of 8). That already beats the best code of the stored sweeps (19.4 mV). An oval (order 2) adds almost nothing (16.9 mV). Order 3 halves the error, to 8.9 mV (IoU 0.93, part score 7.97): only one cell too many, just above the nose. Orders 0–4 and 0–5 remove it (8.58 and 8.51 mV, IoU 1.0, part score 8.0). Orders 0–6 score 8.9 mV. They include every code of orders 0–5, so the larger code is harder to search, not less able. No random allowed code of any size comes near: their median is about 25.5 mV and their best 20.6–22.4 mV.

Finding 2

The face is a moment the tissue passes through, not a pattern it keeps

The score is the best single iteration after release, so training was free to find a code whose tissue passes through the face once. That is what it found. The best code (orders 0–5), run for 20,000 iterations, shows the face's shape (structural IoU ≥ 0.9) once, for 65 iterations (2,155–2,219), and never again. Every size from 3 up behaves the same: one visit of 55–119 iterations, between iterations 2,113 and 2,263. A hundred iterations either side, the score is 9.6 and 18.1 mV; five hundred either side it is back near 28 mV, worse than the tissue with no clamp at its best. The moment is also tuned finely to the code. Codes a step of about 0.007 in G_pol / G_ref away score a median of 16–20 mV at orders 3–6, and at most 8 of 32 stay within 1 mV of the best. As the earlier reports found, the tissue never settles after release. The code chooses the path it takes, and training found a path that passes through the face.

The storyline

The story so far, in plain terms

The ring code is a graphic equaliser. Order 0 is the volume knob: it makes the whole tissue darker or lighter but cannot place anything. Order 1 tilts treble against bass, top against bottom; with it the tissue already passes through a rough face, with eyes above and a blob below. Order 2 adds little. Order 3 is the slider that matters: with it the ring can be high along the top and at a single spot in the middle of the bottom edge, and low on the lower sides. Four numbers then suffice. Two more tidy the last stray cell.

But the face is a cloud that looks like a face. After release the tissue drifts through one pattern after another and never settles. Training found the push that makes the clouds line up into a face at about iteration 2,190, for some 65 iterations. Then they drift on and never form it again. Change the push by a hair and the clouds line up at other times and in other shapes.

What this means. A handful of harmonics is enough to write a face into this tissue, but only as a moment. Making it last would need a different target for training, such as the face held over a whole window, and may need more of the code, or a different hold.

Appendix · supporting analyses

A1 · The trained codes

The best codes of sizes 3 to 6 agree closely. a₀ lies between 0.78 and 0.88, a₁ between 0.21 and 0.26, a₂ between 0.34 and 0.52 and a₃ between −0.32 and −0.15. The higher coefficients are small (|a₄|, |a₅|, |a₆| ≤ 0.12). The trained profile holds the top edge near 1.2, falls to about 0.2 on the lower sides, rises again to about 1.2 at the middle of the bottom edge, and stays near 0.44 at the bottom corners.

A2 · Library starts and random starts

Half the restarts started from the best codes already simulated (the library), half from a random allowed code. The library starts carried the order-3 code forward to orders 4–6, but random starts found the face too: at order 3 the best random start ended at 9.1 mV (population 64), against 8.9 mV for the best library start.

A3 · Features and background

Balanced RMS weighs the 14 feature cells and the other 107 cells equally. The tissue with no clamp gets the background nearly right (8.2 mV) and the features badly wrong (43.6 mV). The dial alone trades one for the other by darkening everything. The trained faces of order 3 and up bring both down together.

A4 · How sharply the score depends on the code

The landscape of best-moment scores is jagged. Around each trained code, a step of 0.01 of each coefficient's half range changes the score by several millivolts in either direction.

A5 · When the best moments fall

From order 3 up, every restart that ends within 1 mV of its size's best has its best moment between iterations 2,015 and 2,196. At orders 1 and 2 most fall between 1,879 and 1,983 (one at 2,969). Order 0's falls just after release, at iteration 309, while the tissue is still dark from the hold.

A6 · Implementation

The code and its limits. A code of orders 0–N is N + 1 coefficients; the 40 held values are the cosine terms summed at each ring cell's angle. The ceiling, every held value between 0 and 1.3, is 80 linear conditions, so each coefficient's allowed range comes from linear programming. They cannot be read off by hand: from order 3 up, a₁ can reach ±0.75, beyond the ±0.65 of a tilt alone, because the higher orders can flatten the ends of the ring. The allowed codes fill only a small part of the box those ranges span (8% at orders 0–3, 3% at orders 0–4), so the search moves any code it draws outside them to the nearest allowed code, and charges the move against the score.

The search. CMA-ES, written out in learnBoundaryHarmonics11x11.py after Hansen's tutorial, with 16 codes per generation (64 in the second round) simulated as one batch of the model. One batch of 16 takes about 6 s for 3,000 iterations. The batched model reproduces the stored single-code sweeps to 10⁻¹² mV. Every code evaluated, with its score, best moment, pattern there and 2000–2999 average, is saved with its restart.

Starting points. The library holds every code already simulated that fits the code being trained: the stored sweeps' codes whose terms point straight up or down (read as signed coefficients), each with its top-to-bottom mirror image, and every code of earlier restarts, padded with zeros for the higher orders. The same rule serves any number of orders and any target.

Deviations from plan. Orders 5 and 6 and a second round with population 64 were added after the first round. They test whether order 4 was enough, and whether the step-like progress was the search's limit or the code's. In the first round, orders 0–2 ranked the library by each code's 2000–2999 average only, which left order 1's best code out of order 2's starting points; from order 3 on, trained codes were also ranked by their pattern at their best moment.

Methods

Terms

Lattice, cell
The tissue is an 11 × 11 square lattice of 121 cells, each coupled by gap junctions to its four nearest neighbours; the lattice does not wrap around at its edges.
Ring cells, interior cells
The 40 cells on the outer edge (rows 0 and 10, columns 0 and 10) are the ring cells; the code acts only on these. The other 81 are the interior cells.
Vmem
A cell's membrane voltage in millivolts (mV); lower is more hyperpolarised and drawn darker.
G_pol, G_ref
G_pol is the conductance of a cell's polarising ion channel; G_ref = 1 nS. Values are quoted as G_pol / G_ref. G_pol changes over time in this model.
Hold, release
During the hold, iterations 0–300, every ring cell's G_pol is overwritten on every iteration with its value in the code × G_ref. From iteration 301 (release) the ring cells follow the model's own rules again.
Ring code, order
The values held on the ring: a₀ + a₁ cos θ + a₂ cos 2θ + … + a_N cos Nθ, where θ is a ring cell's angle about the lattice centre, measured clockwise from straight up. Term n is order n; a code of "orders 0–N" has N + 1 coefficients a₀ … a_N. Order 0 is the dial (the same value on every ring cell). A positive a_n raises n evenly spaced points of the ring, one of them straight up; a negative a_n raises the n points between them.
Mirror symmetry, phase 0
Every term is a cosine measured from straight up (phase 0), with a coefficient of either sign. Such a code is the same on the left and right halves of the ring, as the face is, and every code with that symmetry can be written this way.
Ceiling, allowed codes
Every held value must lie between 0 and 1.3, the range in which the 301-iteration hold changed most smoothly in the dial sweep. For a code of orders 0–N this is 80 linear conditions on the coefficients, so the allowed codes fill a convex region (a polytope).
Target
−60 mV on the 14 feature cells (two 2 × 2 eyes, a three-cell nose and a three-cell mouth) and −5 mV on the other 107 cells, ring included. −5 mV is the most depolarised Vmem a cell can reach (the depolarising channel's reversal potential), so the target is reachable in principle.
Balanced RMS, score
Half the RMS (root-mean-square) error over the 14 feature cells plus half the RMS error over the other 107 cells, in mV, so the small feature set weighs as much as the rest. A code's score is the lowest balanced RMS at any single iteration from release to iteration 2,999 (its best moment). Lower is better.
Feature RMS, other RMS
The two halves of the balanced RMS, before halving.
Structural IoU
Of the interior cells below −34.6 mV (dark), the overlap with the 14 feature cells divided by their union: 1 for exactly the face's cells dark. −34.6 mV is the threshold of the earlier face reports.
Part score
Coverage + separation − 2 × spurious, at most 8. Coverage sums, over the four parts (each eye, the nose, the mouth), the share of the part's cells that are dark (0–4). Separation counts the covered parts that sit in a dark region of their own (0–4). Spurious is the share of the other interior cells that are dark.
No clamp
The same model and starting state with no hold at all, scored the same way.
Stored sweeps, best sweep code
The dial sweep and the Tilted Dial's order-1, order-2 and combined sweeps, all run earlier with the same hold. The best sweep code is the best of those rerun to seed training, scored as here.
Random allowed code
A code drawn uniformly from the allowed codes (see hit-and-run below).
Restart, generation, population
One restart is one independent training run. Each generation it simulates a population of 16 codes (population 64 in the second round) and moves its search toward the better ones.
Library start, random start
Half the restarts begin at one of the best codes already simulated (the library); the other half at a random allowed code.
Near codes
Codes a small random step from the best code: each coefficient moved by a Gaussian step of 0.01 of half its allowed range (about 0.007 in G_pol / G_ref), then moved back into the allowed codes if needed.
Within 1 mV of the best
An iteration at which the best code's balanced RMS is no more than 1 mV above its best score. A visit is a run of consecutive such iterations.

Procedures

Model and simulation
Checkpoint 1888's model and starting state (Vmem −9.2 mV, G_pol / G_ref 1.0, G_dep / G_ref 1.5 on every cell; gap-junction strength 0.05; bioelectric field on, screen size 4, transduction weight 1000, gain −1, bias 0.0005, time constant 10), with its own trained ring values discarded. Time step 0.01, 3,000 iterations per run. Many codes are simulated side by side as separate samples of one batched model; each follows exactly the trajectory of its own single run (checked against the stored sweeps to 10⁻¹² mV).
Coefficient ranges
The smallest and largest value of each coefficient over the allowed codes, by linear programming. The search works on each coefficient divided by half its range (scaled units).
Training (CMA-ES)
Covariance matrix adaptation evolution strategy: each generation draws a population from a Gaussian, keeps the better half, and moves the Gaussian's centre, width and shape toward them. Starting width 0.3 scaled units. A drawn code outside the allowed codes is simulated at the nearest allowed code, and its score is raised by 10 mV per scaled unit it had to move. A restart stops after 150 generations (100 in the second round), or earlier once the search width falls below 10⁻⁴ scaled units.
Library
Every code already simulated that is a phase-0 cosine series of the orders being trained: the stored sweeps' codes whose terms point straight up or down, and every code evaluated by earlier restarts, each also with its top-to-bottom mirror image (a_n → (−1)ⁿ a_n, pattern flipped), which the lattice's symmetry gives without simulation. Lower-order codes enter with their higher coefficients at 0. The 16 library codes whose stored pattern scores best are rerun and scored properly, and library restart r starts from the (r / 2)-th best. In the first round, orders 0–2 ranked the library by each code's 2000–2999 average only; from order 3 on, trained codes were also ranked by their stored best-moment pattern.
Rounds and schedule
First round: population 16, 20 restarts per code size, orders 0–N for N = 0 to 6 run one size after another so each could start from the codes of the sizes below. Second round: population 64, 10 restarts per size for N = 1 to 6, starting from everything the first round simulated.
Random allowed codes
Hit-and-run sampling: from the centre of the allowed region, 500 steps each along a random direction to a uniformly chosen point of the chord through the region.
Checks of the best codes
The best code of each size rerun for 20,000 iterations and scored at every iteration; 32 near codes and 64 random allowed codes scored as in training.
