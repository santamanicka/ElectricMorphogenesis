Free or New?

The earlier reports read patterns from a fixed window, iterations 2000–2999. After the hold the clamped tissue runs by exactly the same rules as the free tissue, so a pattern that looks new in that window might be the free tissue's own pattern at some other time. This report follows the free tissue for 50,000 iterations and the dial and tilted-dial clamps for 20,000, and compares them one snapshot at a time.

Finding 1

The clamped runs do not replay the free run

If a clamped tissue simply rejoined the free tissue's path at some other point, each of its snapshots would have a free snapshot almost identical to it. To know what "almost identical" means here, I ran the free model once more from a start 1 mV higher on every cell. That run does rejoin the free path: within 100 iterations its difference from the free run shrinks to 0.01 mV, and up to iteration 10,000 each of its snapshots lies a median of 0.058 mV from some free snapshot. 95% lie within 0.44 mV, which I take as the tolerance for a replay. Finding 2

…but their patterns are the kind the free model makes from other starting states

After release a clamped tissue obeys the free model's rules exactly. The clamp only decides the state the tissue is in when those rules take over. So the fair comparison is with free runs started elsewhere. I ran 20 free runs from random starting states. Each has the same symmetry as the clamps: every cell and its rotated and mirrored partners get the same starting Vmem and G_pol. Finding 3 · the tilted dial

Strong tilts leave the dial's patterns behind, but go no farther than free runs with the same symmetry

The Tilted Dial report held the ring at DC + G·cos(θ − φ). Its claim was that the tilt reaches patterns the dial alone never makes, but that rested on averages. Here each of its 16 gallery codes (the most typical code of each common motif) is run for 20,000 iterations. Every snapshot is compared with the free run and with the dial alone at eleven DCs (0, 0.52–0.84 and 1.3), at any time. Below, each code's pattern at iteration 2,500 sits next to the closest pattern any dial-alone run ever shows.

Three ways at φ = 0°: tilts, the dial alone and free runs

The five tilts at φ = 0° are compared here with two groups at once. The first is the dial alone, 11 runs. The second is free runs whose starting state keeps the tilt's left-right mirror. These come in three kinds. 21 start with all 8 symmetries of the square (the free run and the 8-symmetry random starts). 20 start from random states with only the mirror. The last 20 are tilted starts, with a tilt in G_pol and Vmem like the one a tilt code leaves; these are the fairest match for the tilts. The question is whether the tilts' patterns are farther from the free model than free runs are from each other.

The storyline

The story so far, in plain terms

The worry. The free tissue never settles. If its patterns went round a fixed loop, a clamped tissue might only jump onto that loop at a different point, and a pattern that looked new in the readout window would be an old one seen at an unusual time.

What the free tissue actually does. Think of a river that meanders forever and never retraces its bends. Over 50,000 iterations the free tissue keeps finding new patterns: a typical moment is 5.8 mV from anything it shows more than 300 iterations earlier or later. There is no fixed loop to jump onto.

The clamped tissues do not rejoin it. Nudge the river's source by 1 mV and the water flows straight back into the same channel, matching it to hundredths of a millivolt. Clamp the edge and the water cuts a channel of its own. It never runs along the free river's channel, early or late, and comes no closer than a few tenths of a millivolt even for a moment.

But it is the same landscape. Start the free river from twenty other symmetric springs and each cuts its own channel too, across the same kind of ground. The clamped channels look just like those. The clamp does not make the tissue do anything the free model cannot. It chooses where the river starts, and so which of the model's countless paths the tissue takes.

Tilting the edge. A gentle tilt moves the spring only slightly, and for brief spells the water runs back into the dial's own channel. A stronger tilt cuts channels the dial never runs through, not even for a moment. Some of the Tilted Dial's gallery patterns, though, were never a place the water passed through at all. They were a blur of several passing moments, made by averaging.

Symmetry decides how far a run can wander. Picture a valley with a single path down its middle. A spring on the midline (a start with all 8 symmetries) sends the water down that path, so these runs, dial runs included, keep meeting the same scenery. A tilt moves the spring off the midline, and so does a free start that keeps only one mirror. Either way the water is free to spread across the valley floor, and each run finds scenery no other run sees. The tilts' new patterns are new in exactly this way, and no more so than any free run from a lopsided spring. One thing does set the tilt apart from a lopsided free start: the clamp's imprint on the edge. Wipe the edge back to what a free tissue would have there and the water takes another course (appendix A6). Nudge the whole tissue by 1 mV instead and it mostly finds its way back to the tilt's channel.

What this means for the earlier reports. Their "new patterns" are not the free tissue's patterns at another time. But a pattern read from one window is a moment on a path that keeps moving. Even the free run's own 1,000-iteration averages differ from one another by 12 mV (median over pairs of windows), more than the 5–7 mV by which the dial runs' readout averages differ from the free run's. A code sets the path the tissue takes and so what it shows at iterations 2000–2999, not a pattern the tissue settles into and keeps.

Appendix · supporting analyses

A1 · The free run never repeats itself

For every 100th iteration of the free run, the distance to the nearest of its own snapshots at least 300 iterations earlier.

A2 · The control: a 1 mV nudge that the free run absorbs

A3 · Symmetry holds for the first few thousand iterations, then breaks

A4 · The averaged readouts

The earlier reports compared 1,000-iteration averages at iterations 2000–2999. Is a dial run's average there the free run's average over some other window?

A5 · Checks

Each dial run's 2000–2999 average matches the dial sweep behind the earlier reports to within 5 × 10⁻⁸ mV, so these are the same simulations. The random starting states are exactly symmetric: 21 distinct values, one per class of cells the square's symmetries carry onto each other. Their Vmem runs from −55 to −5 mV and their G_pol / G_ref from 0 to 1.5.

A6 · The G_pol a tilt leaves on the ring still steers the tissue after release

At release a tilt run is simply a free tissue in a particular state. So the free model can reach every tilt pattern: start it from that state. The useful question is which part of the state carries the pattern. The test takes each tilt's state at release and changes one thing. Ring reset puts the ring's G_pol back to the free run's at the same iteration, which wipes the clamp's direct imprint. The interior keeps everything the hold left in it. A second version also resets the ring's Vmem; it gives the same result to within 0.02 mV. Nudge, the control, keeps the whole state but raises every cell's Vmem by 1 mV. Restarting from the untouched state reproduces the tilt run to within 2 µV.

A7 · Free runs from tilted starts, at all three directions

Each tilt code was also compared with the 60 tilted-start free runs: 20 each at 0°, 45° and 22.5°, each keeping the symmetry of its direction. Every snapshot after release was compared with every tilted-start snapshot, at any time.

Methods

Terms

Lattice, cell
The tissue is an 11 × 11 square lattice of 121 cells, each coupled by gap junctions to its four nearest neighbours; the lattice does not wrap around at its edges.
Ring cells
The 40 cells on the outer edge: rows 0 and 10, columns 0 and 10. The clamp acts only on these.
Interior cells
The other 81 cells, rows and columns 1 to 9.
Vmem
A cell's membrane voltage in millivolts (mV); lower is more hyperpolarised and drawn darker.
G_pol, G_ref
G_pol is the conductance of a cell's polarising ion channel; G_ref = 1 nS. Values are quoted as G_pol / G_ref. G_pol changes over time in this model, so a cell's state is its Vmem and its G_pol.
Dial, hold, release
A dial clamp overwrites every ring cell's G_pol with one value × G_ref on every iteration of the hold, iterations 0–300. From iteration 301 (release) the ring cells follow the model's own rules. "Dial 0.6" is the run held at 0.6.
Free run
The model with no clamp at all, from its standard starting state: Vmem −9.2 mV and G_pol / G_ref 1.0 on every cell. Run for 50,000 iterations.
Control
The free run started with every cell's Vmem 1 mV higher (−8.2 mV), otherwise identical.
Random-start free runs
Twenty unclamped runs from random starting states with the square's symmetries: the 121 cells fall into 21 classes that the 4 rotations and 4 mirrors of the square carry onto each other, and each class gets one Vmem drawn uniformly from −55 to −5 mV and one G_pol / G_ref from 0 to 1.5 (seeds 0–19).
Tilt code, twin
A ring held at DC + G·cos(θ − φ), where θ is a ring cell's angle about the lattice centre, clockwise from straight up. The 16 codes tested are the most typical code of each motif in the Tilted Dial gallery. A code's twin is the dial alone at the same DC.
Symmetry-matched starts
For the tilted codes, 60 more free runs from random starting states with only the symmetry a tilt keeps. There are 20 each: a left–right mirror (as a tilt at 0°), a mirror about the diagonal through the top-right corner (as at 45°), and none (as at 22.5°).
8-symmetry, random mirror and tilted-start free runs
In the three-way comparison at φ = 0°, free runs are split by their start. 8-symmetry: the free run and the 20 random starts with all 8 symmetries (21 runs). Random mirror: the 20 random starts with only the left–right mirror. Tilted starts: the 20 tilted starts at 0°. The last two together are the mirror-only free runs.
Tilted start
A free run whose starting state carries a tilt over the whole tissue. G_pol / G_ref = DC + G·p and Vmem = V + A·p, where p is a cell's offset from the centre along direction φ in units of the half-width. p equals cos(θ − φ) at the middle of each edge. DC and G are drawn from the tilt codes' ranges, 0.52–0.84 and 0.12–0.58. V is drawn from −45 to −15 mV, and |A| from 0 to 20 mV with either sign. Small noise with the tilt's own symmetry is added (up to ±0.1 in G_pol / G_ref and ±5 mV). There are 20 at each of 0°, 45° and 22.5°.
Motif share
For one run, the share of its readout-window snapshots whose motif some other run of a given group shows at any single moment, counting rotations and reflections as the same motif.
Averaged motif
The motif of a code's 2000–2999 average: what the Tilted Dial report showed. It need not appear in any single snapshot.
Snapshot
The Vmem of all 121 cells at one iteration.
Distance
The RMS (root-mean-square) difference between two snapshots: the square root of the mean over the 121 cells of the squared difference in Vmem, in mV. A snapshot that is not symmetric is compared in all 8 of its rotated and mirrored images, keeping the smallest distance.
Nearest free pattern
The free-run snapshot, among all 50,000, at the smallest distance from a given snapshot. For the free run itself, snapshots within 300 iterations of its own are skipped.
Replay, replay tolerance
A snapshot replays the free run when it lies within 0.44 mV of some free snapshot. 0.44 mV is the 95th percentile of the control's nearest-free distances over iterations 301–9,999, when the control is known to follow the free run's path.
Pooled free runs
The free run and the twenty random-start runs together, 450,000 snapshots. When a random-start run is the one being tested, it is left out of the pool (held out).
Motif
The set of interior cells below −29.3 mV (the single-cell saddle, the unstable point between a lone cell's two resting states) in one snapshot.
Cross-recurrence map
A grid of distances between one run at each iteration (rows) and the free run at each iteration (columns), every 20 iterations. A run that follows the free run's path with a delay d draws a dark line at column = row + d.
Readout window, window average
Iterations 2000–2999, and each cell's mean Vmem over them: the readout of the earlier reports. A free window average is the free run's mean over any 1,000 consecutive iterations.
Symmetry departure
The largest difference, over all cells, between a snapshot and its quarter-turned or left–right mirrored image.
Periods
Iterations 301–1,999 (just after release), 2,000–2,999 (the readout window), 3,000–4,999, 5,000–9,999 and 10,000–19,999.

Procedures

Model and simulation
Checkpoint 1888, with its parameters unchanged (gap-junction strength 0.05; bioelectric field on, screen size 4, transduction weight 1000), replayed with the call sequence of the earlier reports. Time step 0.01. Vmem saved at every iteration, G_pol every 10.
Search
Nearest snapshots found by exact search over every saved iteration of the libraries. The dial runs and the control were tested at every iteration after release against the free run, and every 10 iterations against the pooled runs. The random-start runs were tested every 10 iterations.
Averages
Each dial run's 2000–2999 average compared with the free run's average over every 1,000-iteration window (starting at every iteration from 0 to 49,000). Null: each free window average against the nearest free window average that does not overlap it (windows every 10 iterations).
