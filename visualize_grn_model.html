Bioelectric-Morphogen-Gene Facial Patterning Model

Integrated Multi-Scale Developmental Pattern Formation
0. System Architecture Overview
Multi-Scale Hierarchical Integration
The model integrates three timescales: (1) Bioelectric pattern (field-mediated stigmergic model) establishes spatial Vmem, (2) Ca²⁺ transduction (intermediate) converts voltage to biochemical signals, (3) Morphogen gradients (slow) create spatial cues, and (4) Gene expression (slowest) combines morphogen signals with Ca²⁺ gating via AND logic. The bioelectric gate permits (bio_gate ≈ 1.0) or suppresses (bio_gate ≈ 0.0) morphogen-driven gene expression in a spatially specific manner.
1. Gene Regulatory Network Topology
Morphogen-Driven GRN with Bioelectric Gating
The GRN consists of three morphogen gradients (SHH, FGF8, EDN1) that drive the expression of eight genes organized into feature-specific cascades. Eye genes form a 4-level cascade (rx→six3→pax6→lhx2), while nose (alx) and mouth (dlx→hand2) have simpler architectures. Bone (runx2) is the default state. Bioelectric gating modulates all gene expression via Ca²⁺-dependent AND gates.
2. Bioelectric Pattern and Ca²⁺ Transduction
Bioelectric Signals: Vmem → Ca²⁺ → Bio-Gate
Vmem (left): Stigmergic bioelectric model converges to spatial pattern (~-55mV at features, ~-5mV elsewhere). Ca²⁺ (center): Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels transduce Vmem to biochemical signal (normalized). Bio-Gate (right): Ca²⁺ threshold creates binary gate: high (≈1.0) where Ca < threshold (permits genes), low (≈0.0) where Ca > threshold (suppresses genes). Features appear at LOW Ca²⁺.

5. Pattern Flow: GRN-Only vs. Bioelectric Gating
Dual-Driver Integration Improves Spatial Precision
Top row (GRN-Only): Morphogens alone drive gene expression, producing coarse features with imprecise boundaries.
