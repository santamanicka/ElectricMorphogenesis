The Fifth Order

The face code that steers the tissue from its boundary had four terms. A retrained code has five. Every analysis run on the four-term code was repeated on the five-term one and scored against criteria written down beforehand.

The two faces

The fifth term is small. Its coefficient is −0.058, against 0.25 to 0.82 for the first four, and the two ring codes are almost the same curve. Most of what follows is about whether the analyses behave as if that term were not there.

Finding 1

Most of the four-term story carries over

64 of 74 registered criteria give the same result at order 4 as at order 3.

The face forms with all 14 face cells dark and no stray (overlap 1.00; order 3: 0.93). Turning the field feedback off removes every dark cell at both orders. Releasing the ring at 100 iterations already writes 99% of the whole-ring lead of face over background (order 3: 97%). The relay cannot be simplified by averaging blocks of cells, with all eight coarse-graining criteria agreeing. The steering rule from the boundary passed its confirmation test on 320 new codes: 8 of 9 criteria, including the four required ones.

Two registrations fall below the 80% bar set for each of them: the causal-story criteria (5 of 7) and the steering confirmation (7 of 9). In the causal story, the upper wall alone now re-times the sweep (E2a holds) and the upper wall's push on the eyes is gone (E3 fails: −0.012 against +0.192 at order 3). In the confirmation, the interaction between the two steering modules (C5) fails and the reversed lower channel being against the face (C7) now holds.

Finding 2

A small term with a large effect, and steering modules that add up more simply

Removing the fifth term changes the ring by 0.042 (root mean square, in units of the reference conductance), a fifth of the next smallest change. The registration predicted it would be the weakest handle on the face. It is not: the score goes from 8.6 mV to 29.7 mV, as bad as removing order 3 (28.9) and worse than removing order 1 (24.1) or order 2 (24.8). A random change of the same size gives 22.9 mV, so the term matters more than its size alone would suggest.

The two steering modules, a channel along the lower side of the ring and a push from its top, still go with the face. The cell with both is worth less than before: 42.5% of its codes are face-like against 61.3% at order 3, while the other three cells barely moved. The interaction between the modules fell from 0.24 to 0.01. Full faces (overlap 0.5 or more) are more common at order 4: 19 of the 400 sweep codes against 9, and 6 of the 320 confirmation codes against 1.

The picture in simple terms

Think of the 81 interior cells as a sandbar and the 40 ring cells as the shoreline. While the shoreline is pinned, the water on the sandbar is set from its edge. When the shoreline is let go, the tide, which is the cells' own shared field and conductance, takes over and the pools settle where the last ebb leaves water.

Giving the shoreline a fifth, tiny ripple leaves the course of the tide almost the same: the same phases at the same times, with a somewhat smaller lead of face over background (0.28 against 0.36). The two things the shoreline does to the tide still go with the face. Two details differ. The tiny ripple matters as much as the big ones, so the face is held in place by it. And having both of the shoreline's actions is worth less than before.

The picture does not claim a mechanism for why the fifth term matters, which was not tested. It has no travelling waves and nothing outside the tissue. It says nothing about the time the shoreline is pinned beyond what A1 and A2 show.
